# EPISODE 9 — THE HOSTILE WITNESS
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Sophie
**Third voice:** Dysmorphia
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Soft light. No numbers. No behaviours. No debate about her body. The mirror is opposing counsel. Silence is care.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Body image. Dysmorphia. A history we are not going to itemise. No numbers. No commentary on anyone's body. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode nine. Sophie.

SOPHIE: If you glance at the mirror I will end the episode and also the friendship for about six minutes.

ADAM: I will look at the plant. The plant has done nothing wrong.

SOPHIE: The plant is neutral. Rare in this flat.

ADAM: The empty chair is angled at the glass like it's about to cross-examine it.

SOPHIE: It is. It always is. Introduce it or it will introduce itself, and it is not charming.

---

**THE WITNESS**

SOPHIE: Precise. Exhausting. Holds a standard the room keeps failing. Has never once said good enough. That phrase is not in the vocabulary. The mirror is not a mirror. It's a hostile witness with excellent lighting.

DYSMORPHIA: I am the only one being honest.

SOPHIE: You are the only one being cruel and calling it honesty. There's a difference. Lawyers know it. You don't.

DYSMORPHIA: Kindness lies. Friends lie. The glass doesn't.

SOPHIE: The glass is a piece of furniture with an agenda. I have lived with you long enough to know an agenda when it withholds a compliment.

ADAM: What did it take to learn the mirror isn't objective.

SOPHIE: About a decade. And a daily reminder, which is embarrassing, like having to tell yourself the sky is up. Some mornings the reminder sticks. Some mornings the house wins and I leave the room. Leaving is not vanity. Leaving is refusing to sit in a courtroom.

ADAM: What do people do that makes it worse.

SOPHIE: Compliments aimed at the battlefield. "You look well" said like a verdict. Debating the witness on my behalf, as if I haven't already had that argument in every bathroom since I was fifteen. Concern that is actually surveillance. I can feel the glance. The witness loves the glance. It treats it as a second opinion.

DYSMORPHIA: A second opinion that agrees with me.

SOPHIE: A second opinion you forged.

---

**CARE**

ADAM: What does care look like, then. Because I have done the compliment and I have done the silence and I have guessed wrong at both.

SOPHIE: Care looks like talking about my day. My work. The bad joke I made on the bus. Anything that is not the surface. Care looks like not staring, and not performing not-staring so hard that it becomes a new kind of stare. Care looks like letting me leave a room without an inquiry. The inquiry is how the witness gets a microphone.

ADAM: Silence felt like neglect to me.

SOPHIE: Silence about my body is the opposite of neglect. It's you refusing to join opposing counsel. You can still talk. Just not about the evidence the witness keeps inventing.

DYSMORPHIA: If they don't mention it, she will think they noticed and were too polite. I can work with politeness. Politeness is guilt.

SOPHIE: You can work with anything. That's not a skill. That's a parasite with a law degree.

ADAM: When did the name arrive.

SOPHIE: A clinician said the witness wasn't a truth-teller, it was a condition. I didn't believe them. Belief came later, in small boring repetitions, not in a speech. I still don't believe it every day. I believe it often enough to stay in some rooms. That's the current score. Don't clap. It's not a finish line.

---

**THE CONTRACT**

ADAM: Do you want it gone.

SOPHIE: I want a mirror that behaves like furniture. I want to get dressed without a cross-examination. I want the people I love to be bored of this topic forever. Gone is a fantasy where I wake up and the witness has moved out and left a note. It doesn't leave notes. It leaves the light on.

DYSMORPHIA: I stay because I am the last honest voice.

SOPHIE: You are the loudest voice. Honesty is a different job. You weren't hired for it.

ADAM: Say the contract anyway.

SOPHIE: You don't get to sum me up. You don't get my friends as witnesses. You don't get numbers, and you don't get a debate. If I look away on purpose, that is also a kind of looking, and it counts. The people in this room are not your jury. They are my life. You can sit in the glass. You cannot run the house.

DYSMORPHIA: I'll be here when she looks back.

SOPHIE: Then I'll look at the plant. The plant is winning today.

ADAM: A dinner. Someone who loves you tries to help and makes it worse. What do they say, and what did you need instead. No details of the fight with the mirror. Just the table.

SOPHIE: They say you look well, with eyes that are doing an inspection. Or they say they're worried, which puts me on trial in a restaurant. Or they change the subject so hard it becomes the subject. What I needed was the story about their awful boss, and a chair that wasn't a witness stand, and nobody watching me arrange a plate like it was evidence. I can do a dinner. I can't do a dinner that is secretly a hearing.

DYSMORPHIA: If they don't watch, they don't care.

SOPHIE: If they watch, they feed you. I'm picking the version where my friend tells me about their boss. You can starve.

ADAM: What do you hide so the evening can happen.

SOPHIE: How loud the witness is on the way there. How much of me is spent before I sit down. The smile I put on so the table doesn't become a rescue. I hide it because if I bring it, someone will try to win, and winning is just another person arguing with a mirror on my behalf. I don't want a champion. I want a conversation that lets me be a person with a job and a joke.

DYSMORPHIA: The joke is a distraction from the truth.

SOPHIE: The joke is the truth that I am still here and not only your client. Write that down. You love writing things down.

ADAM: What should we never do again, in plain language.

SOPHIE: Don't assess. Don't compliment the surface. Don't ask if I'm okay in a tone that means the body. Ask if I'm okay the way you'd ask anyone, and accept the answer. If I change the subject, follow me. Following me is the care. Staying on the subject is the cross-examination.

DYSMORPHIA: She'll come back to me later.

SOPHIE: Maybe. And I'll have had the garlic bread first, which is a victory so small you could lose it in a drawer. You don't get to annex the garlic bread. The garlic bread was funny. I was funny. You were the lighting package nobody booked.

DYSMORPHIA: Garlic bread is not a counterargument.

ADAM: I was about to say you looked happy in that story.

SOPHIE: Don't. Happy is how people smuggle a body comment in a nicer coat. Say the garlic bread was good. Say I told the joke better than you would have. Follow the subject. If you follow me off the glass, that's the care. If you stand there trying to win against a mirror, you've joined the other side and brought dip.

ADAM: Who were you before the witness got the lighting budget. I want her in the tape.

SOPHIE: Funny earlier. Less careful with photos. I wore what I liked because I liked it, not because I'd briefed a lawyer. I still like things. The witness says liking is denial. I think liking is taste, and taste is a self, and I would like my self back even on the days the glass is loud. That's not a before-and-after. That's a preference I'm allowed to keep.

DYSMORPHIA: Taste is how people get it wrong in public.

SOPHIE: Public is where my friends are. I'm not living in a corridor to keep you comfortable. You don't pay rent. I do.

ADAM: If someone listening loves a Sophie and wants a rule they can remember when they're tired.

SOPHIE: Follow the subject she changes to. Don't inspect. Don't rescue. Don't be silent in a theatrical way. Be ordinary. Ordinary is the opposite of a hearing. If you can give her an ordinary hour, you did more than a speech about acceptance ever did. Acceptance speeches are still about the witness. Ordinary is about the person.

DYSMORPHIA: Ordinary fades. I don't.

SOPHIE: Then fade into the furniture like you were hired to. Mirrors are allowed to be quiet. Try it. Revolutionary concept.

ADAM: Before we go. What do you want left in the room when the witness tries to take the last word.

SOPHIE: That I have a day, a job, a joke, and people who followed me off the subject. That the glass is furniture. That silence about my body was care, not neglect. That I don't owe a progress report to be allowed dinner. If the witness speaks after this, it's speaking to an empty courtroom. I'm with the plant. The plant won. Let it stand.

DYSMORPHIA: I'll speak anyway.

SOPHIE: Then speak to the glass. We're not taking questions. Adam, turn the mic toward the door. We're leaving while I still like the ending.

SOPHIE: And if you get it wrong once, don't make the apology about my body either. Say "I joined the wrong side, I won't again," and then tell me about your day. The repair is the subject change. The repair is not a second hearing with nicer lighting. I can forgive a miss. I can't forgive a tribunal that calls itself support.

DYSMORPHIA: They'll slip again.

SOPHIE: Then they'll slip and come back to the plant. The plant is still winning. That's the standard. Not perfection. The plant.

---

**THE PHOTO**

ADAM: Someone tags you. You're in the back of a photo, laughing, and the witness takes the brief before you've even opened it. What happens in the ten seconds. Then what do you want from me if I sent it.

SOPHIE: Ten seconds: I find the flaw the witness already briefed, I leave the chat, I rehearse not going to the thing next week. What I want from you: don't follow up with "but you look great." That sentence is a cross-examination with a smile. Send "this was a good night" and talk about the joke, the song, the burnt garlic bread. If I need to step out of photos for a bit, let me, without a campaign to fix my confidence. Confidence campaigns are still about the glass.

DYSMORPHIA: The photo is evidence. You can't cross-examine evidence.

SOPHIE: You can't cross-examine a Tuesday either, but you do, every morning, in better lighting than the photo had. The photo is a rectangle. My life is the table. I'm staying at the table.

ADAM: Clothes. People think this is about vanity. Correct them without giving the witness a single measurement.

SOPHIE: It's about a narrator that calls every choice a confession. A jumper isn't a confession. It's cold. If I change outfit four times, I'm not vain, I'm in a meeting I didn't schedule. The helpful thing is not "wear whatever." The helpful thing is not standing in the doorway narrating the meeting. Ask me if I want to leave. If I say the blue one, we leave. The meeting can file a dissent. We're already in the car.

DYSMORPHIA: The blue one still has a record.

SOPHIE: Everything has a record. I'm not living in the archive. I'm living in the car. Adam's driving. You're not invited to comment on the passenger seat.

ADAM: One sentence you want in the room more than the witness's last word.

SOPHIE: She made the garlic bread and told the better joke. That's the caption. If you can't write that, don't write anything. Silence was the care. The caption is the upgrade. The upgrade still isn't about the glass.

DYSMORPHIA: Captions are spin.

SOPHIE: Captions are a life. Spin is what you've been doing with a mirror and a straight face. We're done. The plant needs water. That's the closer. Not you.

---

ADAM: Afterward. The people who stay stopped trying to win the argument for Sophie. They refused to join the other side. Next time. Jordan. And the space that will not vacate.
