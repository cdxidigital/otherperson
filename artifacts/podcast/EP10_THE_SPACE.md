# EPISODE 10 — THE SPACE THAT WON'T VACATE
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Jordan
**Third voice:** Grief
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Quiet park. No deadline jokes that punch down. The absence has a seat. Argue gently. Don't rush him.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Grief. The death of a sibling. No cleanup. No timeline. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode ten. Jordan.

JORDAN: You left a gap on the bench.

ADAM: I didn't know if I was allowed to sit in it.

JORDAN: You're not. He is. You can sit on the other side. That's the whole etiquette. People get this wrong at dinners.

ADAM: I'll get it wrong less loudly.

JORDAN: That's the kindest thing you've said all year.

---

**THE OCCUPANT**

ADAM: Is the third person here.

JORDAN: Yes. Quiet. Heavy. Doesn't shout. Sits in the exact space my brother used to occupy and refuses to vacate. I wouldn't ask it to leave. That would be another death.

GRIEF: I don't leave. That is the job.

JORDAN: He says that like it's a threat. It's also the only promise anyone kept.

ADAM: What did the day after the funeral feel like. Not the speech. The day after the speech.

JORDAN: Time declined to resume its old shape. Everyone else kept the calendar. Work. Bins. A birthday for someone who wasn't him. They looked at me like I was being difficult for not updating my software. Grief does not do deadlines. Anyone waiting for me to be over it is waiting for a train that was never scheduled.

GRIEF: There is no station. I told them.

JORDAN: You told me. They heard "give it time," which is a different sentence and a worse one.

ADAM: What did people bring.

JORDAN: Lasagne. God, the lasagne. I was not short of cheese. I was short of a person. A few people sat down and didn't try to fix the air. Those were the useful ones. The others brought advice like it was a spare key. "He'd want you to be happy." Maybe. He'd also want me to stop letting strangers narrate him.

GRIEF: I am not a stranger.

JORDAN: No. You're the proof the love was real. Without you the absence goes clean and therefore false. I know. I've heard your pitch. I even agree with it. I just don't agree that agreement means I can't have a Tuesday.

---

**THE CALENDAR**

ADAM: I asked you once if you were feeling better. I want to put that on the record so I don't get to be the wise friend.

JORDAN: You asked it kindly. It still landed like a performance review. Better than what. Better than the worst day. Better than last month. Better than a man who is not coming back. The question assumes a graph. I don't have a graph. I have a seat that stays empty and a life that keeps happening around it.

ADAM: What should I have asked.

JORDAN: Are you eating. Do you want company that can tolerate silence. Do you want to say his name or not today. Not "are you better." Better is a door people push me toward because the room makes them uncomfortable. I am not their discomfort to solve.

GRIEF: Discomfort means they remember. I approve.

JORDAN: You approve of anything that keeps him present. So do I. I don't approve of being turned into a lesson.

ADAM: Can the missing person be in the room without wrecking the room.

JORDAN: Yes. That's the only version of healing that hasn't felt like a betrayal. He can be mentioned. He can be laughed about. The space stays. I stay. The people who tried to rush me made the space louder. The people who sat in it made it survivable.

GRIEF: Survivable is not the same as finished.

JORDAN: I didn't ask for finished. I asked for a bench and a friend who doesn't fill the gap.

---

**THE CONTRACT**

ADAM: Do you want grief gone.

JORDAN: No. Don't clap, and don't look worried. Gone would mean the love had a finish line. I can live with the shape. I cannot live with the erasure. What I want is for other people to stop waiting for a version of me who is over it. That person is not coming. I don't want him anyway.

GRIEF: Then we understand each other.

JORDAN: We understand each other and you still take up the whole seat. Typical family.

ADAM: Say what you want from the rest of us.

JORDAN: Sit on your side of the bench. Say his name when I say it. Don't say it to prove you're brave. Don't bring a deadline. Don't bring a graph. If I go quiet, the quiet is not a problem for you to solve. It's the sound of me keeping a promise.

GRIEF: I do not leave.

JORDAN: I heard you. I'm still having a life next to you. That's the deal. You don't get to call that disloyalty.

ADAM: Say his name the way you want it said. Not a eulogy. A correction of the record. First name is enough if that's all you want in the tape.

JORDAN: His name was Ben. He was funny in a way that made shops take longer. He stole chips and pretended it was a tax. He is not a lesson about living in the moment. He is a person who should be interrupting this interview and isn't. If you remember one thing, remember I don't need him improved. I need him accurate.

GRIEF: Accurate is why I stay. The cleaned-up version is a stranger.

JORDAN: For once we agree and it doesn't annoy me. Don't get used to it.

ADAM: Anniversaries. People either swarm or vanish. Which is worse.

JORDAN: Vanish. Swarming I can leave. Vanishing makes me think the world updated and I didn't get the patch. A message that says his name and doesn't attach advice is enough. You don't have to bring lasagne. We are still rich in lasagne. We are poor in people who can tolerate the date on the calendar without trying to flip it.

ADAM: What does a good ordinary week look like now. Not healed. Lived.

JORDAN: I go to work. I laugh at something that isn't about him and I don't punish myself for the laugh. I leave the gap on the bench. I talk to a friend who doesn't steer. I have a bad hour and I don't call the bad hour a relapse, because grief isn't a program I failed. Then I eat. The week holds. The seat stays empty. Both.

GRIEF: The empty seat is the true sentence.

JORDAN: The empty seat and the eaten dinner. If you only quote one, you're editing me. Stop editing me. Everyone else already tried.

ADAM: People say time heals. I'm not going to say it. You say what time actually does.

JORDAN: Time makes the ordinary possible around the gap. It does not shrink the gap to fit a social plan. The first year was weather I couldn't name. Later years are a life with a permanent extra chair. I can go to a wedding and also need to leave before the speeches. That isn't regression. That's a person with a real absence attending a real room. If time healed in the way people mean, Ben would become a story I tell neatly. He isn't neat. I don't want him neat.

GRIEF: Neat is a second loss.

JORDAN: Yes. So is a life that refuses every room because you're in it. We're both staying. Don't make me pick.

ADAM: What do I do on the day itself, if I'm lucky enough to still be invited.

JORDAN: Send the name. Not a paragraph of meaning. Turn up if I asked. Leave if I go quiet, without making the quiet a scene. Mention a chip-thief story if I've opened that door. Don't invent a moral. He doesn't need a moral. He needs the seat and the accuracy. You can do accuracy. You've been practising all season. Do it here without a bow.

GRIEF: No bow. Agreed.

JORDAN: Look at us. A consensus. Don't ruin it by calling this progress.

ADAM: If someone is new to this and terrified of saying the wrong name. What do they do instead of vanishing.

JORDAN: They ask. "Do you want his name in the room today." Yes or no is a complete answer. If yes, they say Ben like a person, not like a sermon. If no, they sit on their side of the bench and talk about the traffic. Traffic is allowed. Erasure isn't. Panic isn't care. Asking is care. I can handle a clumsy question better than a careful disappearance. Disappearance is the one that feels like the funeral happening twice.

GRIEF: Say the name.

JORDAN: When I say yes. Not when you want credit. That's the whole rule. It's small enough to remember and big enough to matter. Don't decorate it.

JORDAN: And if you cry, don't apologise for it and don't make me comfort you for remembering him. Cry on your side of the bench. I'll be on mine. That's allowed. What isn't allowed is turning your tears into a reason I should get over it faster so you can feel better. Your feelings can exist. They don't get to set the timetable. Ben doesn't get edited so the room can recover. The room can cope. I have.

GRIEF: Coping is not leaving.

JORDAN: Coping is both of us still here. I said that already. I'm saying it until it sticks with everyone except you. You already know. You're just dramatic about it.

---

**BEN'S CHAIR**

ADAM: There's a chair in your kitchen that isn't really for guests. I'm not going to be clever about it. What do you want people to do when they notice.

JORDAN: Notice, and don't redecorate. Don't move it to make the numbers even. Don't put a coat on it like it's storage. If you sit in it, ask. He was a chip thief, not a shrine that can't bear a living body. The mistake is pretending the chair is empty. The other mistake is pretending it's holy and the rest of us should eat standing up. Ask. Then sit, or don't. Both are kinder than a speech.

GRIEF: The chair is taken.

JORDAN: The chair is his and it's also a chair. You don't get to freeze the kitchen in amber because you miss him. I miss him in a kitchen that still gets used. That's the deal you and I made. Don't breach it in front of my friends.

ADAM: The story you're willing to have told. One. So people stop inventing a eulogy.

JORDAN: He stole chips off every plate within reach and acted offended when you noticed. He laughed with his whole face. He was not a lesson. If you tell that, you've told the truth. If you turn him into a quote about living every day, you've replaced him with a fridge magnet. I don't want a magnet. I want the chip thief at the table, and when that's impossible, I want the story told at the right size.

GRIEF: The right size is the whole room.

JORDAN: The right size is a story and a chair and a Tuesday that still has dinner in it. The whole room was never the brief. You inflate the brief so nobody can meet it. I'm meeting it. Chips. Name. Dinner. That's a faithful life. You can call it small. I call it accurate.

ADAM: If I get the day wrong. If I ask "how are you" like a form. Repair it for me.

JORDAN: Say his name if I've said yes, then ask what I want to eat. Don't spiral into guilt. Guilt makes me host your feelings at a table that's already full. Eat. Stay on your side of the bench. Get the chips. That's the repair. It's embarrassingly practical. He would have respected it. He had no time for a speech when there were chips.

GRIEF: Practical is a way of leaving.

JORDAN: Practical is a way of feeding people. Including me. Including the space you occupy. I'm not arguing this one again. Pass the plate or leave the kitchen. Lovingly.

---

ADAM: Afterward. Jordan's calendar is different from other people's. The ones who stay stopped trying to reset it. Next time. Mei. And the thermostat.
