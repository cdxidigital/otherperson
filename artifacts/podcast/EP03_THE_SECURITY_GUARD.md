# EPISODE 3 — THE SECURITY GUARD WHO NEVER CLOCKS OFF
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Elena
**Third voice:** Trauma
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Kitchen. Hallway light on in daylight. No graphic events. Interrupt. Argue. The guard is loyal and exhausting.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Trauma. Hypervigilance. Body memory. No graphic detail. If you need to leave, leave. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode three. Elena.

ELENA: You left the hallway light on.

ADAM: It was already on. It's the middle of the day.

ELENA: And yet. Sit where I can see the door. Not behind me.

ADAM: Noted. Empty chair by the door. Sightlines. As specified by someone who has never once relaxed in a restaurant.

ELENA: I relax. I just do it facing the exit like a person with taste.

ADAM: Introduce the third person before it introduces itself.

---

**THE GUARD**

ELENA: A very tired security guard who never clocks off. Always standing. Always scanning. Convinced that if it sits down for one minute the original threat will walk back in wearing a different face. Loyal in the most exhausting way possible.

TRAUMA: I heard loyal. I'll take it.

ELENA: You missed exhausting.

TRAUMA: Exhausting is a review. Loyal is the job description.

ADAM: Does it ever sit.

TRAUMA: Sitting is how people get surprised. I don't do surprised.

ELENA: You don't do lunch either. Or friends. Or a film where someone closes a door too loudly.

TRAUMA: Loud doors are information.

ELENA: Loud doors are doors.

ADAM: Who were you before this had a job title.

ELENA: Louder. I danced in kitchens. I assumed tomorrow would be roughly the same shape as today. I thought danger announced itself, like a weather warning, with time to bring the washing in. I didn't know the body could keep a private archive and not tell the owner what the filing system was.

TRAUMA: The archive is the point. Forgetting is how it gets a second chance.

ELENA: Forgetting is also how people have birthdays.

---

**THE MUTINIES**

ADAM: When did you notice it. Not the name. The first mutiny.

ELENA: There wasn't one clean moment. Smoke that wasn't there. Waking up already braced, heart up, for a threat that hadn't arrived. A certain tone of voice emptying a room while everyone else was still chatting about a barbecue. My body started remembering things my mind was still refusing to look at. I got very good at a neutral face while time-travelling without a passport.

ADAM: People thought you were distracted.

ELENA: People thought I was rude. Distracted is the polite version they tell you later. In the moment they think you've left the conversation. I had. I was in a different year and trying to pay for a coffee at the same time.

TRAUMA: And she came back. Because I brought her back. You're welcome.

ELENA: You dragged me back by the collar. That's not a thank-you situation.

ADAM: First time a doctor said the word.

ELENA: Relief and grief in the same breath. Relief that the body wasn't inventing hobbies. Grief that I couldn't keep calling it a quirk I should have outgrown. The name made it real in a way I couldn't flirt my way out of.

TRAUMA: Names are useful. They stop her pretending I'm a personality trait.

ELENA: You are not a personality trait. You are a night shift that forgot to end.

ADAM: I used to think the way you pick a seat was taste.

ELENA: It was architecture. Exits built into every conversation. Back to the wall. Bag on the chair I might need. I have ended friendships with rooms, not with people.

ADAM: That's a hell of a sentence.

ELENA: It's also just Tuesday.

---

**THE ARGUMENT**

ADAM: Chair. What do you want from her.

TRAUMA: Vigilance. Continuous. Unbroken. The past is not finished. If she forgets, it returns without warning. I have seen this movie. I did not like the ending.

ELENA: It thinks it's the only adult in the room.

TRAUMA: I am the only adult in the room. The rest of you are acting like the shift is over.

ADAM: Has it ever been right.

ELENA: Yes. That's the trap. Every now and then it catches something genuine. Those moments become the evidence for the other ninety-nine times it was wrong. It is excellent at selective memory. One true alarm, laminated, on the fridge. The false alarms go in the bin.

TRAUMA: Selective memory is good record-keeping. I keep us alive. She can call it a prison. I call it a security contract with no exit clause.

ELENA: Contracts have lunch breaks.

TRAUMA: Not this one.

ADAM: What do you hide from the rest of us.

ELENA: The flinch that isn't about you. The nights sleep feels like a bad idea. The part of me that still treats kindness as a lead-in, because kindness has preceded harm before and my body files patterns, not character references. I hide how much energy it takes to stay in an ordinary conversation. You think we're talking about a holiday. I am also doing perimeter.

ADAM: I have stood too close behind you.

ELENA: You have. I didn't say it because saying it makes a scene and the guard hates scenes almost as much as it hates surprises. Which is confusing, because it causes both.

TRAUMA: I cause neither. I prevent. There is a difference you keep refusing to learn.

ELENA: You prevent dinner.

---

**ONE HOUR OFF**

ADAM: Do you want it fired.

ELENA: No. And I resent the no. I don't want a world where nothing is watching the door. I want a guard who can sit down for one full hour and not treat the quiet as suspicious. I'm still here. That should count for something.

TRAUMA: People who say I'm still here are usually about to stop checking.

ELENA: Or they are telling you the check worked and you can have a sandwich.

ADAM: What should the people who love you actually do.

ELENA: Don't stand behind me. Don't handle me like I'm glass and then resent the handling. Don't ask me to open rooms I have told you are closed. Stay in the room when I go quiet. The quiet is not a rejection. It is me spending the last of the budget on remaining.

TRAUMA: Remaining is not the same as safe.

ELENA: Remaining is the evidence. You don't get to delete the evidence that doesn't suit you.

ADAM: New contract. Say it.

ELENA: You can watch the door. You cannot veto the table. You can flag a real risk. You cannot laminate one bad year and use it as a blanket ban on every closed door, every kind voice, every man who walks past with a backpack. One hour off the clock a day. Non-negotiable. And if I say I'm still here, you write it down. You don't call it suspicious. You call it the result.

TRAUMA: I'll note it.

ELENA: You'll argue with it. I know. I'm still saying it.

ADAM: Stay with it. A ordinary Tuesday. Not the worst day. The day that looks fine in a photo.

ELENA: I meet a friend for a walk. I pick the route with two exits and a shop I can duck into. We talk about their kid. I laugh. I am also counting footsteps behind us and deciding which of them are irrelevant. When I get home I need an hour where nobody speaks to me, not because I don't love them, because the budget is spent. From the outside that's a nice walk. From the inside it's a shift.

TRAUMA: And the shift kept the walk possible. Say that part.

ELENA: The shift also made the walk smaller than it had to be. Both. I'm not giving you a performance review with only the compliments.

ADAM: What do you still not open.

ELENA: Some rooms. Some years. A couple of questions you are not getting today. The closed door is not an invitation to be a better interviewer. It's a boundary. The guard and I agree on that one, which is rare enough that I should frame it.

TRAUMA: Boundaries are vigilance with better branding.

ELENA: Boundaries are how I stay in the friendship. Vigilance is how I almost leave it. Learn the difference or I'm docking your pay.

ADAM: If the people listening love someone like you.

ELENA: Believe the flinch isn't about them. Don't take a seat behind her. Don't punish the quiet. Don't demand the story as the price of staying. The story is not the entry fee. The staying is.

ADAM: Last argument, then I'll get out of the doorway. What does loyalty from the guard cost the people who aren't a threat.

ELENA: It costs them warmth they earned. A friend reaches for a hug and I step sideways and then spend the night hating the step. They didn't do anything. The archive did. I go back the next day and try to explain without making them hold the whole file. Some can. Some decide I'm cold. Cold is easier for them than true.

TRAUMA: Cold keeps the distance correct.

ELENA: Cold keeps me lonely. Correct and lonely is not the win you think it is.

ADAM: And if they stay after the sideways step.

ELENA: Then they get the second version. The one that can sit at the table if the door is visible and nobody makes a speech about bravery. I don't want to be brave. I want to finish a cup of tea. If they can want that with me, the guard can complain from the hall.

---

**THE SUPERMARKET**

ADAM: Give me a shop. Not a metaphor. A Tuesday shop where the guard clocks on without asking.

ELENA: Aisle three. Someone drops a tray. My body leaves the year we're in. I'm holding oat milk and also holding a younger hallway. The guard wants the exit. I want the oat milk. We argue next to the cereal like a couple who should have stayed home.

TRAUMA: The sound was sufficient. We don't debate sufficient.

ELENA: The sound was a tray. The year was not in the aisle. You imported it. That's not security. That's a very confident mix-up.

ADAM: What do you need if I'm with you and the tray drops.

ELENA: Don't grab me. Don't ask if I'm okay in a voice the whole aisle can hear. Stand where I can see you, say I'm here, and let me finish the sentence with the oat milk. If I need to leave, I'll leave. If I stay, don't congratulate me. Congratulations are for people who climbed something. I bought milk.

TRAUMA: Leaving would have been cleaner.

ELENA: Leaving would have meant no breakfast. I'm tired of a safety plan that doesn't include breakfast.

ADAM: The friends who decided you were cold. What did they miss.

ELENA: They missed the walk back. I step sideways, then I come back the next day with the explanation I can afford, which is not the whole file. If they only score the sideways step, they miss the return. The return is the love. The step is the archive. Cold is what it looks like if you go home before the second scene.

TRAUMA: Second scenes are exposure.

ELENA: Second scenes are friendship. Exposure is you, making every doorway a courtroom. I'm done paying court fees on a hug.

ADAM: One hour off. What does the hour actually contain. Be specific or the guard will turn it into a drill.

ELENA: Phone in the other room. Door locked once, not four times. A show I don't monitor. Tea. If a noise happens, I note it and I do not get up. The guard can file a complaint. The complaint does not get a chair. That's the hour. It is not enlightenment. It is a lunch break I had to legislate.

TRAUMA: An hour is how things get in.

ELENA: An hour is how I stay a person you can have dinner with. Pick a side.

---

ADAM: Afterward. I thought Elena's seating was personality. It was a floor plan. Some rooms she still doesn't open. I didn't push. The guard is still by the door. Elena is still at the table. Next time. David. And the weather.
