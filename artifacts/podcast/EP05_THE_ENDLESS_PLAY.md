# EPISODE 5 — THE ENDLESS PLAY
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Priya
**Third voice:** OCD
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Quiet café. The chair has already been moved three times. Comedy, then the tax. No how-to of rituals.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. OCD. Intrusive sequences. The cost of a mind that will not call cut. No instruction manual. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode five. Priya.

PRIYA: Don't comment on the chair.

ADAM: I wasn't going to.

PRIYA: You were going to. Everyone does. It's been moved. It's wrong. It's been moved again. It's still wrong. We can start anyway or we can live here.

OCD: We should not start until it's right.

PRIYA: We are starting. That's called a coup.

ADAM: Introduce the director.

---

**THE DIRECTOR**

PRIYA: Precise. Exhausted. Holding a script that grows new pages while you watch. Doesn't shout. Just repeats the stage direction until the actor complies out of sheer defeat.

OCD: Compliance is not defeat. Compliance is safety.

PRIYA: Compliance is me being late to my own life.

OCD: Late and alive. Priorities.

ADAM: What does it feel like from the inside. Not the poster. The Tuesday.

PRIYA: Compulsory dress rehearsals of catastrophe. The same terrible play, directed by someone who refuses to close the production. If I don't complete the sequence, the catastrophe becomes more likely. The sequence is never finished. Only paused. It is not a hobby. It is the tax I pay for a quieter mind. Some days the tax is too high. I pay it anyway.

OCD: Forty seconds of relief is still relief.

PRIYA: Then the intern arrives.

ADAM: The intern.

PRIYA: The next cue. Shows up like an unpaid intern who doesn't understand weekends, sick days, or the concept of a human bladder.

OCD: The play does not close because the risk does not close. That is called consistency.

PRIYA: That is called a full-time job I never applied for and cannot resign from.

---

**LATENESS**

ADAM: People think you're unreliable.

PRIYA: People think I'm flaky. I am running a show that will not let me leave the building. If I walk out mid-scene I don't feel free. I feel exposed. The people who stay learned that my version of reliability is showing up after the scene, not before it. That is not disrespect. That is the tax made visible.

ADAM: I have texted "where are you" like you were choosing a longer shower.

PRIYA: I was not choosing. I was being directed. There's a difference, and it doesn't show up well in a blue tick.

OCD: She could have left sooner if she'd done it properly the first time.

PRIYA: There is no first time. You add a page when I reach the end. That's not quality control. That's a hostage situation with a highlighter.

ADAM: Has it ever caught something real.

PRIYA: Once. Maybe twice. Enough for it to laminate the review and ignore the other four hundred performances where nothing happened except me losing an afternoon. One true alarm. Ninety-nine leaves. Same trap as Sarah's clipboard. We should form a union.

OCD: A union would need a quorum and a risk assessment. We don't have time.

PRIYA: We never have time. That's your whole bit.

---

**THE NAME**

ADAM: When did it get a name.

PRIYA: University. A counsellor said OCD and I laughed because I thought OCD was people who like tidy drawers. I like a mess. The mess is not the point. The point is the unfinished catastrophe in my head that will not leave until I pay it in actions I don't even believe in.

OCD: Belief is optional. Completion is not.

PRIYA: I don't believe you. I still pay you. That's the most humiliating sentence I own.

ADAM: What do you hide.

PRIYA: How long it takes. How small the sequence can look from the outside and how large it is from here. The messages I don't send because sending them would start a new scene. The friendships that think I don't care because I arrived after the cake. I care. I was in rehearsals.

ADAM: What would you do with the time if the play closed.

PRIYA: I don't know. That's its own fear. If the job ended I might have to find out who I am when I'm not busy preventing a disaster that mostly lives in the script. The uncertainty is not freedom yet. It's a corridor.

OCD: The corridor is unsafe. Stay in the theatre.

PRIYA: The theatre smells like panic and hand soap. I'm allowed to want a window.

---

**THE CONTRACT**

ADAM: Do you want it gone.

PRIYA: I want the relief to last longer than forty seconds. I want to be on time to something I love without negotiating with a director who isn't even on the call sheet. I don't know how to want it gone. Gone feels like standing on a stage with no lines and an audience that expects me to improvise safety.

OCD: Improvisation is how people get hurt.

PRIYA: So is never leaving the building.

ADAM: Say the contract.

PRIYA: You can cue me. You cannot add pages after the bow. You get one pass, not a trilogy. You don't get to call my friends unreliable witnesses. And if I am late, you don't get to narrate it as my personality. It's your runtime. I am allowed to want a life with a closing time.

OCD: I'll take it under—

PRIYA: Do not say advisement. Sarah already has the trademark.

OCD: Then I'll say the show must go on.

PRIYA: The show can go on without consuming the actor. That's allowed in other industries.

ADAM: Give me the afternoon you lost. Not the sequence. The cost. What didn't happen because the play wouldn't end.

PRIYA: A train. A dinner I had cooked the story of in my head. A friend who said "another time" in the voice people use when there might not be another time. I arrived to the platform of my own evening after the evening had gone home. I stood there with the relief of having finished and the grief of having missed it. Forty seconds of quiet. Then the next cue, as if the loss was irrelevant because the checklist was complete.

OCD: The checklist is the point. The dinner was a risk.

PRIYA: The dinner was a life. You keep replacing my life with risk and expecting a thank-you note.

ADAM: What do the people who stay do differently.

PRIYA: They stop asking me to just stop. If I could just stop, I would have, sometime around the first lost train. They tell me the time they can actually do, not the time they wish I could do. They don't joke that I'm fussy about chairs. The chair is not fussiness. The chair is a scene. Laugh with me, not at the mechanism. And if I'm late, assume the play, not the personality.

OCD: If they accommodate her, the play gets longer.

PRIYA: If they don't, I get lonelier. I know which of those you prefer. I don't.

ADAM: What would you keep, if you could edit the director and not fire the whole mind.

PRIYA: The care. The noticing. The part that doesn't want the people I love to come to harm. I'd cut the part that thinks harm is prevented by me completing a private performance nobody else can see. Care without the hostage situation. That's the fantasy. I'm allowed a fantasy that isn't a catastrophe.

OCD: Fantasies are unmanaged risk.

PRIYA: Then put it in the program notes and let me go home at a human hour.

ADAM: Argue with it about love. It thinks it's protecting the people you love. Tell it what that protection looks like from their side.

PRIYA: It looks like absence. It looks like a friend who cares so much she misses the moment care was needed. You don't protect someone by failing to arrive. You protect a script. They wanted me. The script wanted a private rehearsal of disaster. I picked the rehearsal because you made the other choice feel like negligence. That's the con.

OCD: Negligence is how bad things happen.

PRIYA: So is never showing up. Put both on the clipboard. Sarah's manager would at least admit the metrics are cruel. You pretend yours are kindness.

ADAM: If the play has to exist, where is it allowed to run.

PRIYA: Not through dinner. Not through the train. Not through the first hour of seeing someone I love. It can have a corner of the morning. It cannot have the plot. I'm the protagonist. That used to sound like a joke. I mean it.

OCD: Protagonists who ignore direction get hurt.

PRIYA: Protagonists who obey every direction never get a life. I'm taking the note and leaving the building.

ADAM: Tomorrow morning. Before the first cue. What do you want the director to hear from you, out loud, so it can't pretend it wasn't said.

PRIYA: I am allowed to be late to your scene and early to my life. I am allowed to leave a sequence unfinished and still be a careful person. Care is not the same as captivity. If you cue me before coffee, I am allowed to say not yet. Not yet is not negligence. Not yet is a person. You work for the show. The show does not own the actor. Say it back or sit in the corner and sulk. I'm making the coffee either way.

OCD: Not yet leaves the danger open.

PRIYA: Living leaves the danger open. I'm choosing living. Get in line behind the kettle.

---

**THE FRIEND ON THE PLATFORM**

ADAM: The friend who said another time. Did you tell them why, or did the play narrate you as flaky.

PRIYA: I sent "sorry, running late" four times and then "I can't." I didn't send the true sentence because the true sentence sounds like a bit. There's a scene in my head that won't end. They'd have believed traffic. Traffic is a dignified lie. The play prefers dignified lies. They make me look careless instead of captive, and careless is somehow more socially acceptable.

OCD: Careless is your word. I was being complete.

PRIYA: You were being complete in a hallway while a person stood in a restaurant checking the door. That's not completeness. That's a missed scene you refuse to count because it wasn't on your list.

ADAM: Say the sentence you should have sent. Now. The director doesn't get a rewrite.

PRIYA: I'm not flaky. I got stuck in a loop that felt like leaving would hurt you. I was wrong. Missing you was the harm. I want another time that is a real time, with a start I can survive. If I'm late, it's the loop, not my love.

OCD: If you promise a real time, you owe a perfect arrival.

PRIYA: I owe a honest arrival. Perfect is your religion. I'm agnostic as of this morning.

ADAM: What does a survivable start actually look like. Give the people a prop, not a theory.

PRIYA: They text "leaving now, no rush" instead of "where are you." They pick a place I already know. They don't joke about the chair. They let me arrive mid-scene without a recap that sounds like a charge sheet. And if I cancel, they say tomorrow is still open, once, and then they go eat. The guilt of them waiting is jet fuel. The dignity of them eating is a firebreak.

OCD: Firebreaks let danger through.

PRIYA: Firebreaks let the rest of the city live. I'm in the city. I'm not your only building.

ADAM: Argue with the idea that love means never being inconvenient.

PRIYA: I am inconvenient. So is everyone worth knowing. The loop tells me inconvenience is a moral stain, so I vanish to stay pure. Vanishing is the bigger stain. I'd rather be the late friend who tells the truth than the pure one who never arrives. Purity doesn't hug anyone. It just keeps a clean script and an empty table.

OCD: An empty table is safe.

PRIYA: An empty table is the catastrophe you claim to be preventing. Look at it. That's your work. I'm clearing a plate anyway.

---

ADAM: Afterward. Priya has become an expert in her own mind. The expertise is invisible and expensive. Next time. Tom. And the ex who still has a key.
