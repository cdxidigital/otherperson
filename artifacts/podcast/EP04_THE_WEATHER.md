# EPISODE 4 — THE WEATHER
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** David
**Third voice:** Bipolar
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Afternoon light. Two energies in one chair already arguing about the lamp. Comedy, then the crash. No spectacle.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Bipolar. The high. The crash. Not a personality quiz. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode four. David.

DAVID: You've put one chair out for two people. That's optimistic.

ADAM: They refused to split the booking.

DAVID: They always refuse. One wants the lamp on. One wants the street to go away. I'm the furniture.

ADAM: You look like you've already lived three versions of this afternoon.

DAVID: I have. One of them had a business idea. One of them cancelled the idea and also my personality. This one has a biscuit. We're doing well.

---

**TWO IN THE CHAIR**

ADAM: Introduce them. Both. Don't let the loud one answer for the quiet one.

BIPOLAR: I'll answer. He likes it when I answer. I make him interesting.

DAVID: He likes it until Thursday.

BIPOLAR: Thursday is a rumour spread by people who own calendars.

DAVID: Two of them. One wants confetti and a three-day plan to reinvent me before the shops shut. The other wants the curtains closed and the phone in another postcode. They share a body like a divorced couple forced to co-parent a nervous system. They do not do handover politely.

ADAM: What does the high feel like. No poster version.

DAVID: Like being the most charismatic person at a party only I was invited to. Ideas faster than my hands. Charm turned up until I can convince anyone of anything, including myself. It feels like being switched on. It also forgets there are other people in the room who will still be there when the power cuts.

BIPOLAR: The power doesn't cut. It rests. You're so dramatic about rest.

DAVID: You call a three-day blackout rest. I call it waking up in a different country with no passport and a hangover made of concrete.

BIPOLAR: Both versions are you. Stop picking a favourite child.

ADAM: Do you have a favourite.

DAVID: Everyone else does. They prefer the festival. They text the festival. They make plans with the festival. Then the weather changes and they act like I've breached a contract they never read.

---

**THE NAME**

ADAM: When did it get a name.

DAVID: Late twenties. After a stretch where I redesigned my entire life on a Tuesday and couldn't get off the couch by Friday. A doctor said bipolar and half the room relaxed because there was a map. The other half relaxed because they finally had a short explanation they could give other people without doing any work.

BIPOLAR: The name made the voltage official. I was always the voltage.

DAVID: You were also the apology tour. Don't forget your greatest hits.

ADAM: What did friends do with the name.

DAVID: Some of them treated the high like the real me and the low like a moral failure. As if I'd chosen bad weather to inconvenience a barbecue. The name helped the doctors. It also gave strangers a story they could flatten me into. I am not a forecast. I am a person living inside one.

BIPOLAR: He can take the medication. Narrower is not the same as gone. I don't do mild. Mild is for other people's group chats.

DAVID: Mild is how I keep a job and a friend. You think that's boring. I think that's rent.

ADAM: Do you miss the high.

DAVID: Yes. I hate that I miss it. I don't miss the crash. Most days the trade is worth it. Some days I still miss the festival even though I know the ending, the way you miss a person who set fire to your kitchen but danced really well.

BIPOLAR: You don't apologise for electricity.

DAVID: You do if you leave the iron on and burn the house down. Electricity has manners available. You just refuse them.

---

**A SPECIFIC NIGHT**

ADAM: I was at the festival once. You had a plan. Three businesses. A move. You loved everyone in the room, including a man you'd met eleven minutes earlier.

DAVID: And you drove me home at 2 a.m. because the plan had started to include a tattoo and a one-way flight. Thank you. I was unbearable. I was also, in that hour, convinced I was finally the person I was supposed to be. That's the cruel bit. It doesn't feel fake from the inside. It feels like the truth arriving late.

ADAM: Then the Thursday text. "Can't."

DAVID: That was the other one. Curtains. Phone in the fruit bowl. You thought I was dodging you. I was trying not to let you see the concrete hangover and call it the real me either. Both edits were dishonest. I'm bored of edits.

BIPOLAR: The sunny one is the real one. The other is maintenance.

DAVID: The sunny one booked a flight we couldn't afford. Maintenance cancelled it and also cancelled my ability to answer the door. I need a third setting that neither of you respect. It's called Wednesday. People live there.

ADAM: What should people who love you actually do.

DAVID: Don't only love the sunny days. Don't treat the storm as a character flaw. Check the forecast without trying to control the climate. And when I say I love you at 1 a.m. with a business plan, believe that I mean it and also wait until morning before you co-sign anything.

BIPOLAR: Morning is when he becomes boring.

DAVID: Morning is when he becomes safe to love. Those can be the same man.

---

**THE CONTRACT**

ADAM: Say the new contract.

DAVID: You can bring weather. You cannot spend my money, my sleep, or my friends in a single night. You can be bright. You cannot speak for the version of me who has to live with what you promised. If you want range, you get range with a dimmer, not a detonator. And you don't get to call the crash a moral failure or the high the only true me. Both are mine. Neither is the whole house.

BIPOLAR: I'll consider the dimmer.

DAVID: You'll hate the dimmer. I'm still installing it.

ADAM: Medication. People get weird about the word. Say it plain.

DAVID: It narrowed the range. I can hold a job. I can finish a conversation without launching a second life inside it. I still miss the voltage on some mornings the way you miss a city you can't afford. Anyone who tells me to just be grateful has not stood in the doorway of that city and walked away on purpose.

BIPOLAR: Gratitude is a sedative other people prescribe.

DAVID: And resentment is a sedative you prescribe. I'm not taking either as a full dose.

ADAM: What do you hide from the sunny friends.

DAVID: The crash logistics. Cancelling. The shame after the charm. The money of a bright week. The text I don't send because it would be either a love letter or a disappearance and I can't tell which until Wednesday. I hide how much work the ordinary day is, because if I describe the work they say "but you seem fine," and fine is the costume the weather lends me when it wants the room to relax.

BIPOLAR: The costume is beautiful. Stop badmouthing the costume.

DAVID: The costume doesn't do the dishes. I do. That's the personality. Not the fireworks.

ADAM: If someone loves a David.

DAVID: Love both forecasts or admit you only visited. Don't ask which one is real. Ask what today needs. Sometimes today needs a walk. Sometimes today needs you to not co-sign a plan hatched after midnight. Sometimes today needs you to sit in the flat version without mourning the entertaining one out loud. I can hear that mourning. It sounds like disappointment with a smile.

BIPOLAR: They miss me. That's a market.

DAVID: They miss a highlight reel. I'm not a highlight reel. I'm the whole week. Including the part where I return the library books.

ADAM: One concrete week. Monday to Sunday. No highlight reel.

DAVID: Monday I answer messages like a person. Tuesday the bright one suggests we reorganise the flat, the job, and our personality before lunch. I make toast instead. Wednesday is the good boring day, the one I'd like people to meet. Thursday the dimmer slips and I cancel a drink and tell the truth in one sentence instead of a novel. Friday I sleep. Saturday I see you and I don't perform the festival. Sunday I feel the pull of the old voltage and I don't follow it to the airport in my head. That's a successful week. It will not trend. It kept my life.

BIPOLAR: Toast is not a personality.

DAVID: Toast is how the personality stays housed. You'd have us sleeping in the idea of a house. I've tried that. The idea doesn't have a roof.

ADAM: If the dimmer fails for a week. What do you want, specifically, not inspirationally.

DAVID: A text that doesn't require a performance. Food that arrives without a speech. No one asking if I'm "back." Back implies I left the building. I didn't. The weather moved through. Check on Wednesday me, not only on the fireworks. And if I start pitching a new life after midnight, tell me you love me and that we're talking about it tomorrow. Tomorrow is the whole safety plan. It's boring. It's why I still have a house.

BIPOLAR: Tomorrow is a delay tactic.

DAVID: Tomorrow is how adults don't set fire to the week. You'll live.

---

**THE GROUP CHAT AT 1 A.M.**

ADAM: Read me the text you didn't send. The 1 a.m. one. Paraphrase. I don't need the novel.

DAVID: It was three novels. One was I love you and we should start a business. One was I'm sorry I've been strange, please don't leave. One was a list of ways to become a different man by Friday. I sent none of them. I sent "you up?" which is the weather pretending to be casual.

BIPOLAR: You up is efficient. It opens the night.

DAVID: You up is how I set fire to a Tuesday from inside a Monday. Adam, if you get you up from me after midnight, the correct reply is: I love you, go to sleep, we'll talk at nine. Not a business plan. Not a feelings tribunal.

ADAM: What if I'm also awake and I want the festival version. Be honest. I have wanted him.

DAVID: Then want him in the morning, on a walk, with a budget. The festival version is real. He is also a terrible accountant. If you only show up for him, you are dating a highlight reel and invoicing the rest of me for the damage. I can feel that invoice. It arrives as you seem flat today.

BIPOLAR: Flat is an insult. He used to be electric.

DAVID: Electric blew the fuse. Flat keeps the fridge running. I would like my friends to be on the side of the fridge.

ADAM: Money. You said the bright week has a cost. Say it without the speech.

DAVID: A bright week buys things future me has to return, apologise for, or quietly pay off. The crash then treats the debt as proof I shouldn't be trusted with joy. It's a very neat trap. Joy becomes evidence against me. So I start hiding the good days too, in case they turn into receipts.

BIPOLAR: Receipts are your hobby, not mine.

DAVID: Receipts are the weather's tab. I just get the bill.

ADAM: Wednesday me. If he walked in right now, what would he order.

DAVID: A coffee. A normal conversation about your terrible taste in jumpers. No pitch. No disappearance. He'd leave when he said he'd leave. That man is not boring. He is the one who still has friends in a year. If you can't love him, you don't love me. You love the trailer.

BIPOLAR: The trailer is why they sat down.

DAVID: The series is why they stay. Learn the difference or give the remote back.

---

ADAM: Afterward. David has lit rooms up and emptied them. The people who stay stopped asking which version is real. They both are. Next time. Priya. And the play that will not close.
