## EPISODE 0 — PROLOGUE
**Title:** The Other Person in the Room

**ADAM:** Content note. This series talks about mental illness, trauma, addiction, grief, body image, panic, and low mood. No graphic detail. Skip what you need. Support lines are in the show notes.

**ADAM:** There is something deeply stupid about discovering that someone you have known for years has been living with a person you have never met.

You know their laugh. You know how they take their coffee. You know the version that answers the phone and says I’m fine with exactly enough conviction to make you stop asking.

What you do not know is that the whole time there has been someone else in the room. Sitting slightly behind them. Holding a list. Editing the script.

I am Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room.

Most books and podcasts about mental illness ask: what is wrong with you. This one asks: what is it like to live with you. Then it goes one step further. I interview the person. Then I interview the thing they have been living with.

An illness cannot sit in a chair. It cannot roll its eyes. But the people I love describe these things in human terms. Anxiety warns. Depression negotiates. Trauma keeps receipts. OCD runs dress rehearsals of catastrophe. Addiction bargains like an ex who still has a key.

Sometimes they protect. Sometimes they punish. Sometimes they become so familiar that life without them feels like moving out of your own house.

I started with a simple question. If your mental illness were sitting in this room with us right now, what would it look like.

**SARAH:** That’s easy. Mine would be sitting behind me. Slightly to the left. Holding a clipboard. Looking disappointed that I still haven’t double-checked the stove.

**ADAM:** Why behind you.

**SARAH:** Because if I could see its face properly I’d probably punch it. And it knows that.

**ADAM:** That was the moment this stopped being an interview and became an introduction.

Over the next twelve episodes I sit with twelve friends, and with the third person in the room. The humour is there because the situation is often absurd. The hard edges are there because the cost is real. This is not a smear campaign. It is the side of life we usually only sense.

Episode one. Sarah. And the clipboard.

## EPISODE 1 — SARAH + ANXIETY
**Title:** The Clipboard  
**Content note:** Chronic anxiety, hypervigilance, constant risk assessment.

**ADAM:** Content note. Anxiety and hypervigilance. I’m Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode one. Sarah.

We’re in a backyard. Late afternoon. Cold tea. Her dog is using her foot as a pillow. I have left an empty chair slightly behind her and to the left. As requested.

Sarah, the anxiety is in the chair. What does it look like today.

**SARAH:** Same as always. Clipboard. Disappointed face. The energy of a project manager who has never once taken annual leave and resents everyone who has.

**ADAM:** When did it first show up.

**SARAH:** Eleven. School camp. Dining hall. Plastic tray. Suddenly I am certain that if I eat the food I will choke and everyone will be annoyed at me for making a scene. Not worried. Certain. Full lighting. Most kids get homesick. I got a risk-assessment department opening overnight. The voice sounded practical. Like a very anxious middle manager. We should probably just not eat, to be safe. Also the building looks flammable.

**ADAM:** What does a normal day actually feel like.

**SARAH:** Continuous low-grade maths. Probability running in the background while I answer an email. Did I lock the door, and if I did, did I see myself lock it or only think I did. The checking is not drama. It is the only way I know to keep the people I love safe. I know it doesn’t work that way. I still do it. Fine is the most successful disguise it ever built for me.

**ADAM:** I’m turning to the chair. What do you want from her.

**ANXIETY:** Certainty. Full, laminated, preferably notarised certainty that nothing bad will happen on her watch. Is that so much to ask.

**ADAM:** Yes.

**ANXIETY:** Rude. She keeps trying though, which is why I keep her employed.

**SARAH:** It literally just called me staff.

**ANXIETY:** You are staff. The job is prevent catastrophe. The KPIs are impossible. I still expect overtime.

**ADAM:** What do you tell her at three in the morning.

**ANXIETY:** That the previous fifty safe moments are statistically irrelevant. That the next one is the dangerous one. That happiness is when people get sloppy. I cannot have sloppy.

**SARAH:** It has never once said well done on the fifty safe moments.

**ANXIETY:** Why would I. That’s the baseline. You don’t get a medal for not burning the house down.

**ADAM:** Has it ever been right.

**SARAH:** Annoyingly, yes. Once every few years it catches something real, and then it walks around like it invented risk assessment. The rest of the time it’s screaming about leaves.

**ANXIETY:** I keep the receipts for the times I was useful. I delete the rest. That is called data-driven decision making.

**SARAH:** That is called being an arsehole with a filing system.

**ANXIETY:** Semantics.

**ADAM:** Do you want it gone.

**SARAH:** I want it quieter. I want it to sit in the corner instead of standing in front of the door. I want the clipboard to have fewer pages.

**ANXIETY:** I’ll take it under advisement. No promises.

**ADAM:** Afterward. I used to tease Sarah about checking the stove from the driveway. I thought it was a quirk. It was a full-time unpaid security job she never applied for. When she cried once, she apologised. The apology felt like another rule the clipboard had written.

## EPISODE 2 — MARCUS + DEPRESSION
**Title:** The Landlord  
**Content note:** Depression, numbness, isolation, persistent low mood.

**ADAM:** Content note. Depression and isolation. Adam James. The Other Person in the Room. Episode two. Marcus.

Blinds open for once. Two coffees actually being drunk. Empty chair that already looks exhausted.

Marcus, introduce your housemate.

**MARCUS:** Tired. Older than me. Sits like it’s paying rent on the chair and I’m the difficult tenant. Landlord energy. Given up on renovations. Just wants everyone to stop asking for things.

**ADAM:** When did it move in.

**MARCUS:** Sixteen. Kitchen. Father left. Door closed and the house went quiet in a way that felt structural. I waited for sadness or rage. Instead there was thick, heavy nothing. I thought it was shock. Shock is meant to pass. This one signed a lease. I told everyone I was tired for twenty years. Tired is the Swiss Army knife of cover stories. Very effective. Also completely full of shit.

**ADAM:** What does it say when it’s strong.

**MARCUS:** It doesn’t shout. That’s the scam. It waits until you agree. Effort is pointless. You’re already behind. People would be better off if you stayed still. It’s like being gaslit by your own nervous system.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What do you want from him.

**DEPRESSION:** Rest. Permanent rest. Stop performing competence. Stop hoping tomorrow will be different. The hope is frankly embarrassing for both of us.

**MARCUS:** It’s very polite about the emotional demolition. That’s the worst part.

**DEPRESSION:** I’m doing him a favour. Without me he’d have to believe the emptiness is personal. This way it has an explanation. You’re welcome.

**MARCUS:** I hate that I sometimes find that comforting.

**ADAM:** Do you want it gone.

**MARCUS:** I want the weight lifted. I also don’t know who I am without the explanation. Both things can be true. That’s the most annoying sentence in the entire arrangement.

**DEPRESSION:** I’m almost merciful. In a completely unhelpful way. That’s my brand.

**ADAM:** What do you wish people knew.

**MARCUS:** When I go quiet it is not because I don’t care. It is because caring feels like lifting something heavier than I can currently lift. Stay anyway. Don’t invent hobbies for me.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Marcus used to stay until the last bottle was empty. Then he started leaving after forty minutes. We invented normal reasons. I invented them too. The silence after I asked what the nothing felt like in the body was the most honest answer he gave.

## EPISODE 3 — ELENA + TRAUMA
**Title:** The Security Guard Who Never Clocks Off  
**Content note:** Complex trauma, PTSD, hypervigilance, body memory. No graphic descriptions of events.

**ADAM:** Content note. Trauma and hypervigilance. No graphic events. Adam James. Episode three. Elena.

Kitchen. Hallway light on in daylight. Empty chair with sightlines to every exit.

Elena, who’s in the chair by the door.

**ELENA:** A very tired security guard who never clocks off. Always scanning. Convinced that if it relaxes for even a second the original threat will walk back in wearing a different face. Loyal in the most exhausting way possible.

**ADAM:** When did you notice it.

**ELENA:** Not one clean moment. A series of small mutinies. Smoke that wasn’t there. Waking up already braced. A certain tone of voice emptying a room. My body started remembering things my mind still refused to look at. I got very good at keeping my face neutral while time-travelling without consent. People thought I was distracted. I was in a different year.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What do you want from her.

**TRAUMA:** Vigilance. Continuous. Unbroken. The past is not finished with us. If she forgets, it will return without warning. I have seen this movie. I did not like the ending.

**ELENA:** It thinks it’s the only adult in the room.

**TRAUMA:** I am the only adult in the room. The rest of you are out here acting like the war is over.

**ADAM:** Has it ever been right.

**ELENA:** Yes. That’s the trap. Every now and then it catches something genuine. Those moments become the evidence it uses to justify the other ninety-nine times it was wrong. Excellent at selective memory.

**TRAUMA:** I keep the receipts for every time I was useful. I delete the rest. That is how survival works.

**ELENA:** And that is how a prison gets built out of old alarm systems. I don’t want it fired. I want it to sit down for one full hour. I’m still here. That should count for something.

**TRAUMA:** It counts as suspicious. People who say I’m still here are usually about to stop checking.

**ADAM:** Afterward. I used to think the way Elena positioned herself in rooms was personality. It was architecture. She had built exits into every conversation. Some rooms she still doesn’t open. That silence was left intact.

## EPISODE 4 — DAVID + BIPOLAR
**Title:** The Weather  
**Content note:** Bipolar mood states, mania, depressive crashes.

**ADAM:** Content note. Bipolar mood states. Adam James. Episode four. David.

Living room. Afternoon light. Two energies occupying the same empty chair and already disagreeing about the lighting.

David, how many people are fighting in that chair.

**DAVID:** Two. One wants confetti and a three-day plan to reinvent the self. The other wants the curtains closed and the phone in another postcode. They share a body like a bitter divorced couple forced to co-parent a nervous system.

**ADAM:** What does the high feel like. What does the crash feel like.

**DAVID:** The high feels like being the most charismatic person at a party only I was invited to. The crash feels like waking up in a different country with no passport and a hangover made of concrete. Both versions are real.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What do you want from him.

**BIPOLAR:** Range. Full range. The quiet version of him is safer and also deeply boring. I bring the weather. Sometimes the weather is a festival. Sometimes it’s a blackout. I don’t do mild.

**DAVID:** Mood stabilisers narrowed the range. I missed the high. I did not miss the crash. Most days the trade is worth it. Some days I still miss the festival even though I know how it ends.

**BIPOLAR:** I notice the blackout. I simply refuse to apologise for the voltage. You don’t apologise for electricity.

**ADAM:** What do you want people to understand.

**DAVID:** Both versions of me are real. Don’t only love the sunny days. Don’t treat the storm as a moral failure. Loving me means making room for the weather.

**BIPOLAR:** He can call it weather. I call it being fully switched on. The dimmer switch version is for other people.

**ADAM:** Afterward. David has lit up rooms and emptied them. The people who stay have learned to check the forecast without trying to control the climate.

Next time. Priya. And the endless play.

## EPISODE 5 — PRIYA + OCD
**Title:** The Endless Play  
**Content note:** OCD patterns, compulsory sequences, cost of rituals.

**ADAM:** Content note. Obsessive-compulsive patterns. Adam James. Episode five. Priya.

Quiet café. Empty chair already adjusted three times and still not correct.

Priya, introduce the third person.

**PRIYA:** Precise. Exhausted. Holding a script that grows new pages while you’re watching. Never shouts. Just repeats the stage direction until the actor complies out of sheer defeat.

**ADAM:** What do the sequences feel like from the inside.

**PRIYA:** Compulsory dress rehearsals of catastrophe. If I don’t complete the sequence, the catastrophe becomes more likely. The sequence is never finished, only paused. It is not a choice I enjoy. It is the tax I pay for a quieter mind. Some days the tax is too high. I pay it anyway.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What do you want.

**OCD:** Completeness. The sequence must be finished or the danger remains active. I am the only one treating the threat with the seriousness it deserves.

**PRIYA:** The relief after finishing lasts approximately forty seconds. Then the next cue arrives like an unpaid intern who doesn’t understand weekends.

**OCD:** Forty seconds of safety is still safety. You’re welcome. The play does not close because the risk does not close. That is called consistency.

**PRIYA:** That is called a full-time job I never applied for and cannot resign from. The sequences make me late. They make me seem unreliable. The people who stay learn that reliability just looks different on me.

**OCD:** She can call it a disorder. I call it quality control. Someone has to maintain standards.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Priya has been forced to become an expert in her own mind. The expertise is exhausting and largely invisible.

## EPISODE 6 — TOM + ADDICTION
**Title:** The Ex Who Still Has a Key  
**Content note:** Alcohol addiction and recovery. No detailed accounts of using.

**ADAM:** Content note. Addiction and recovery. No using detail. Adam James. Episode six. Tom.

Park bench. Empty space that still feels like it has a key to the house.

Tom, is the third person still here.

**TOM:** An ex who still has a key and occasionally tries the door. I don’t let it move back in. I know the sound of its footsteps in the hallway.

**ADAM:** How did the bargain start.

**TOM:** Fun at the beginning. Charismatic. Made everything easier, louder, more possible. Then it started rewriting the lease. The next drink will be different. This time the terms will be mine. They never were.

**ADAM:** To the space. What did you offer him.

**ADDICTION:** Relief. Immediate. Reliable. Temporary. I solved the feeling when nothing else was working. The terms were always mine. He just stopped reading the fine print.

**TOM:** Recovery is not a single dramatic turning point. It is a thousand small, deeply unsexy decisions. Celebrate the boring parts. They are the ones that keep the door locked.

**ADDICTION:** He can call it a problem now that he’s sober and boring. I call it the only solution that ever showed up on time and didn’t ask for a conversation first. I can wait. I am extremely patient. And I have excellent timing.

**TOM:** I know. That’s why the ordinary Tuesdays matter more than the crisis nights.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Tom’s recovery is quiet and daily. The people who only celebrate the dramatic version miss the actual work.

## EPISODE 7 — AISHA + PANIC
**Title:** National Emergency  
**Content note:** Panic attacks, sudden bodily emergency, false alarms that still feel life-threatening.

**ADAM:** Content note. Panic attacks. Adam James. Episode seven. Aisha.

Living room. Door visible. Empty chair that already feels like it’s vibrating.

Aisha, who’s in the room with us.

**AISHA:** Loud. Certain. Completely uninterested in proportion. Treats a slightly elevated heart rate like the building is on fire and the exits are blocked by bees.

**ADAM:** What does it feel like when it arrives.

**AISHA:** National emergency declared by a body that did not consult me. A supermarket aisle can become a disaster movie in under ten seconds. The false alarms still require full evacuation. Very committed to the bit.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What do you want.

**PANIC:** Evacuation. Now. I do not do maybe later. I do not do let’s assess. I do full-scale emergency and I do it with confidence. This time is different. It is always this time. The previous fifty times being wrong is irrelevant data.

**AISHA:** Don’t tell me to calm down. That is the least helpful sentence in the English language. Help me get somewhere safe until the false alarm finishes its shift. Presence without matching panic is the only useful offering.

**PANIC:** I will finish when I finish. Your schedule is not my problem.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Aisha has left rooms that looked, from the outside, like ordinary rooms. They were not ordinary to her nervous system.

## EPISODE 8 — LIAM + REJECTION SENSITIVITY
**Title:** Advanced Pattern Recognition  
**Content note:** ADHD-related rejection sensitivity, intense responses to perceived criticism or abandonment.

**ADAM:** Content note. Rejection sensitivity. Adam James. Episode eight. Liam.

Café. Empty chair already interpreting this conversation as a potential breakup.

Liam, introduce the third person.

**LIAM:** Hyper-alert. Treats ordinary feedback like the opening scene of a tragedy. Means well in the most relationship-destroying way possible.

**ADAM:** What does it actually feel like.

**LIAM:** I need a bit of space arrives in my nervous system as I am leaving forever and also you were never enough. I have pre-emptively ruined things that were not actually ending. Impressive talent. I would like to return it.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What are you trying to do.

**REJECTION SENSITIVITY:** Protect him. If I react first, the abandonment cannot blindside us. Better to end it myself than wait for the inevitable. The evidence is always there if you look closely enough. I look closely enough for both of us.

**LIAM:** Translate feedback like you’re speaking to someone whose nervous system is convinced every sentence contains a hidden exit. I am listening. It just doesn’t look like it from the outside.

**REJECTION SENSITIVITY:** I am simply reading the subtext correctly. He can call it sensitivity. I call it advanced pattern recognition with a strong bias toward survival.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Liam’s mind moves quickly. The sensitivity moves faster. The people who stay have learned to slow the translation.

## EPISODE 9 — SOPHIE + DYSMORPHIA
**Title:** The Hostile Witness  
**Content note:** Body dysmorphia, negative body image, history of disordered eating. No behaviours or numbers.

**ADAM:** Content note. Body image and disordered eating history. No behaviours or numbers. Adam James. Episode nine. Sophie.

Soft light. Empty chair angled toward the mirror like a hostile interviewer.

Sophie, the critic is present.

**SOPHIE:** Precise. Exhausting. Holds a set of measurements that reality keeps failing to meet. Has never once said good enough. That phrase is not in its vocabulary.

**ADAM:** What is the mirror like.

**SOPHIE:** A hostile witness. It has never been a neutral party. Excellent lighting for cross-examination. Learning that the mirror is not objective took about a decade and still needs daily reminders.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What do you want from her.

**DYSMORPHIA:** Accuracy. I am the only one seeing clearly. Everyone else is being kind or delusional or both. The measurements do not lie. Kindness does.

**SOPHIE:** The people who love me have learned the most useful thing is silence about my body. Not compliments. Not encouragement. Just refusing to join the opposing counsel. That silence is care.

**DYSMORPHIA:** She can call it a disorder. I call it the last honest voice in the room.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Sophie has spent years in negotiation with a surface that was never going to give her peace. The people who stay stopped trying to win the argument for her. They just refused to join the other side.

## EPISODE 10 — JORDAN + GRIEF
**Title:** The Space That Won’t Vacate  
**Content note:** Complicated grief following the death of a sibling.

**ADAM:** Content note. Grief and the death of a sibling. Adam James. Episode ten. Jordan.

Quiet park. Empty space beside him that feels more occupied than most people.

Jordan, is the third person here.

**JORDAN:** Yes. Quiet. Heavy. Permanent. Doesn’t shout. Just sits in the exact space my brother used to occupy and refuses to vacate. I wouldn’t ask it to leave. That would be another death.

**ADAM:** What did the day after the funeral feel like.

**JORDAN:** Time simply declined to resume its previous shape. Everyone else kept the old calendar and looked at me like I was being difficult for not updating. Grief does not do deadlines. Anyone waiting for me to be over it is waiting for a train that was never scheduled.

**ADAM:** To the space. What do you want.

**GRIEF:** Continuity. I am the proof the love was real. Without me the absence becomes clean and therefore false. I stay so the love still has somewhere to go. You’re welcome.

**JORDAN:** Sit with me instead of trying to move me along. The missing person can be present without destroying the present. That is the only version of healing that hasn’t felt like betrayal.

**GRIEF:** I do not leave. That is the entire job description.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Jordan’s calendar is different from other people’s. The ones who stay have stopped trying to reset it to the old time.

**Title:** The Thermostat  
**Content note:** Social anxiety, fear of judgement.

**ADAM:** Content note. Social anxiety. Adam James. Episode eleven. Mei.

Corner table. Empty chair already running probability calculations on every possible sentence.

Mei, your risk-assessment department is in session.

**MEI:** Alert. Exhausted. Holding a clipboard of every possible wrong sentence and the precise social death that would follow. Trying to keep me safe from humiliation. Succeeding at keeping me very small.

**ADAM:** What happens when you enter a room with more than three people.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What’s the goal.

**SOCIAL ANXIETY:** Safety through silence and accuracy. Every sentence is a potential exit wound. Better to say nothing than to say the thing that makes them leave. I am the thermostat. I keep the temperature low enough that no one gets burned. Including her.

**MEI:** Thank you for staying in the room even when I’m quiet. The quiet is the sound of me choosing to stay. It’s expensive.

**SOCIAL ANXIETY:** She can keep practising the thing that feels like dying until it only feels like severe discomfort. I will still be here measuring the risk. That is called thoroughness.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Mei has mapped rooms most people walk through without thinking. The people who stay have learned that her silence is often the sound of her staying.

## EPISODE 12 — ALEX + THE LOW VOICE
**Title:** The Question That Stays Open  
**Content note:** Persistent depression and passive suicidal ideation. No plans, methods, or active intent. Focus on the daily decision to stay.

**ADAM:** Content note. Persistent low mood and passive suicidal ideation. No plans. No methods. Focus on staying. Adam James. Episode twelve. Alex.

Simple room. Empty chair that doesn’t need volume to dominate the space.

Alex, who’s in the chair.

**ALEX:** Persistent. Not theatrical. Doesn’t need to raise its voice. Just keeps the question open like a low-grade radio station that never quite goes off air. Questions the point. Not with plans. Just with the steady suggestion that staying is optional and possibly overrated.

**ADAM:** To the chair. What do you actually want.

**THE LOW VOICE:** Perspective. I am the question that keeps him honest. Without me he might start believing the effort is easier than it is. I don’t demand an ending. I only refuse to let the question close.

**ALEX:** I stay anyway. That is also true. Both realities live in the same body. The most useful sentence anyone ever gave me was: you don’t have to feel better to stay. You just have to stay. Still paying rent on that sentence.

**THE LOW VOICE:** He answers the question every day by remaining. The answer is his. The question remains mine. I’m consistent like that.

**ALEX:** Don’t try to argue it into silence in one conversation. Just be one of the reasons staying feels slightly more possible than the alternative. Presence without pressure. That’s the only intervention that has ever worked. The low voice is not the whole truth. I stay. That is also the truth.

**ADAM:** Afterward. Alex’s decision to stay is daily and quiet and largely invisible. The people who love him have learned that the most important support is remaining available without requiring a performance of feeling better.

## EPISODE 13 — FINALE
**Title:** The Other Person Was Me

**ADAM:** Content note. This finale gathers the whole series. Same subjects. Same care.

I am Adam James. This is the last episode of The Other Person in the Room.

The interviews changed the way I understand my friends. They also forced me to look at the rooms I have been living in without introducing the occupants. Fear. Grief. Shame. The parts of myself I have never quite known how to name.

What if everyone has an other person in the room. Not necessarily an illness. Perhaps just the version of themselves they have never formally met.

I asked each of the twelve one last question. If you could introduce the person you are now to the person you were before all of this began, what would you say.

Their answers were different. The shape of the answers was the same.

You survived.  
The other person did not get the whole house.  
You are still here.

**SARAH:** I want the clipboard quieter. Not gone.

**MARCUS:** Stay anyway.

**ELENA:** I’m still here. That is the part that matters.

**DAVID:** Loving me means making room for the weather.

**PRIYA:** Reliability looks different on me.

**TOM:** Celebrate the boring parts.

**AISHA:** Presence without matching panic.

**LIAM:** Translate gently. I am listening.

**SOPHIE:** Silence about my body is care.

**JORDAN:** Sit with me. There is no deadline.

**MEI:** The quiet is the sound of me staying.

**ALEX:** You don’t have to feel better to stay. You just have to stay.

**ADAM:** The illnesses may occupy rooms. They may rearrange the furniture. They may turn the lights off. They may sometimes convince the person there is no door.

But they are not the whole house.

A diagnosis can describe a person’s experience without becoming their identity.

The other person may always be in the room.  
But they don’t necessarily get to decide who lives there.

I’m Adam James. Thank you for sitting in these rooms with us.
