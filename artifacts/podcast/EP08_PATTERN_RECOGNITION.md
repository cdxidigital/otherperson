# EPISODE 8 — ADVANCED PATTERN RECOGNITION
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Liam
**Third voice:** Rejection Sensitivity
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Café. Fast, then catching himself. The third voice is already planning the breakup. Argue. Translate. Don't diagnose the friendship to death.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Rejection sensitivity. ADHD in the mix. Ordinary feedback arriving like a funeral. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode eight. Liam.

LIAM: You've been quiet for four seconds. Are we breaking up.

ADAM: We've been recording for four seconds.

LIAM: That's how it starts. A pause. A look. A man with a podcast deciding I'm too much. I've already drafted the apology and the exit.

REJECTION: He's not wrong to draft it. Pauses are information.

LIAM: Pauses are people thinking. You would criminalise thinking.

ADAM: Introduce it before it files the divorce.

---

**THE SCANNER**

LIAM: Hyper-alert. Treats ordinary feedback like the opening scene of a tragedy. Means well in the most relationship-destroying way possible. If you say "I need a bit of space," my nervous system hears "I am leaving forever and also you were never enough."

ADAM: I have said I need a bit of space.

LIAM: And I heard a eulogy. I then sent you six messages explaining why I was fine, which is how you know I was not fine.

REJECTION: Six messages is due diligence. If you leave first, the abandonment cannot blindside you.

LIAM: If I leave first, I abandon myself and call it strategy. We've been over this.

ADAM: Give me a normal example. Not a film.

LIAM: A mate says "that joke didn't land." A normal brain hears a note. Mine hears a tribunal. By the time I've smiled, I've also inventoried every time I've been embarrassing since 2009. It's impressive. I would like to return the talent.

REJECTION: The evidence is there if you look closely enough. I look closely enough for both of us.

LIAM: You look so closely you invent clues. A short reply is not a clue. A short reply is someone on a bus.

REJECTION: Buses are where people decide to leave.

LIAM: Buses are where people decide to sit down. You have never caught a bus. You don't know.

---

**THE RUINED ALMOST**

ADAM: Tell the one you pre-emptively ruined. The almost.

LIAM: There was someone. Not a grand love. A good ordinary thing. They said they were tired and wanted an early night. I heard the ending. I sent a calm, insane paragraph about how I understood if this was too much, and that I'd rather know now. They were actually tired. I turned a bedtime into a breakup they then had to talk me out of. We didn't survive the talking-out. I did the leaving by trying to prevent the leaving.

ADAM: That's grimly funny.

LIAM: It's funny in a pub. It's not funny at 11 p.m. when you've just fired a kind person for yawning.

REJECTION: Better to end it than wait. Waiting is how you get hurt.

LIAM: Ending it is also how you get hurt. You just get to be the one holding the pen. You love the pen.

ADAM: What does useful feedback sound like now.

LIAM: A second sentence. "This is about the joke, not about you disappearing." I know that's a lot of labour. It's less labour than watching me invent an ending and then act as if it already happened. Translate gently. I am listening even when it looks like I'm defending. The defence is the scanner, not the person.

REJECTION: The second sentence is a trick. The ending is still in there.

LIAM: Sometimes it isn't. That's the part you keep getting wrong, and I keep paying for.

---

**THE NAME**

ADAM: When someone finally believed you.

LIAM: A clinician said the sensitivity was real, not me being dramatic for sport. Half the treatment was being believed. The other half was doing the boring thing where I don't send the paragraph for twenty minutes. Twenty minutes is a long time when your brain is on fire and also writing a very articulate email.

ADAM: Do you want the speed gone. The creativity.

LIAM: No. I want the part that treats every note like a death. I'd keep the fast mind. I'd return the funeral director. If I could separate them. I'm not sure I can. They share an office and the funeral director has the louder phone.

REJECTION: I am advanced pattern recognition with a strong bias toward survival. He can call it sensitivity. I call it not being stupid twice.

LIAM: You are stupid in a very alert way. That's still stupid.

ADAM: Say the contract.

LIAM: You can flag a real exit. You cannot draft one from a pause, a short text, or a yawn. You wait twenty minutes before we publish anything. You don't get to speak to my friends in the voice of a tragedy. And if they stay, you write that down too. Staying is data. You don't only file the endings.

REJECTION: I'll keep the file.

LIAM: Then add a tab called nothing bad happened. It's going to be the long one. Shocking, I know.

ADAM: Let's do one of mine, since I offered. I replied "sounds good" to a long message you sent about plans. Four words. What did the scanner do with it.

LIAM: It held a funeral. In a church. With seating. "Sounds good" meant fine, fine meant fading, fading meant you'd upgraded to someone with fewer admin needs. I drafted three follow-ups. I sent none, which is growth, and also why my notes app looks like a breakup that never got a second party. Then I spent the bus ride building the case for why you'd be right to leave. The case cited sources. The sources were a yawn from March.

REJECTION: The case was strong.

LIAM: The case was a hobby. He was on a job site. He has a job. Not everything is a referendum on my right to exist.

ADAM: I was on a roof. I liked the plan. Sounds good meant sounds good. I hate that you had to do archaeology on four words.

LIAM: I hate it too. Don't do the guilt monologue or the scanner will decide the guilt means you're about to leave out of pity, which is its favourite genre. Next time, add four more words. "Sounds good. Thursday still works." That's not poetry. That's a handrail. People survive stairs because of them. They don't write songs about them. They just don't fall down the shop.

REJECTION: Handrails are for people who expect to fall.

LIAM: Handrails are for stairs. Stairs are not a moral failure. God, you're tiring.

ADAM: What do you want to keep from the fast mind.

LIAM: The jokes. The way I can see a room. The loyalty, which is real even when it dresses up as panic. I'd like the loyalty without the pre-emptive goodbye. If someone is actually leaving, I can survive that. I've survived worse than a short text. What I can't keep surviving is breaking things that were still intact because I wanted to be right about the ending.

REJECTION: Being right is safer than being surprised.

LIAM: Being right and alone is not a prize. I've checked the prize table. It's empty. Sit down.

ADAM: What do you want said back to the person you almost fired for yawning. If you could get the sentence right now.

LIAM: I mistook tired for leaving. That was my scanner, not your character. You didn't owe me a trial. If there's still a door, I'll knock like a normal person and not like a closing argument. If there isn't, I'll survive that without making you the villain of a film you weren't shooting.

REJECTION: Too soft. They'll hear weakness.

LIAM: They'll hear accuracy. Weakness is burning a kind thing to feel safe for an hour. I've done the audit. The safe hour wasn't worth the month.

ADAM: And for the people still in the room with you.

LIAM: If I go quiet after a short text, it's not punishment. It's me doing the twenty minutes. You can send the handrail. You don't have to love the scanner. You have to talk to me like the ending isn't already written. I can meet you there. I just can't invent the second sentence on your behalf every time. That's how I become the director of a tragedy with no cast.

REJECTION: I'll still draft it.

LIAM: Draft it. We're not publishing. New house rule. The drafts can scream in the other room.

ADAM: Give the scanner a ordinary win. Something it filed wrong that you can prove. I want it on the record so it can't edit the tape later.

LIAM: You sent "sounds good" from a roof and still turned up on Thursday with the plan intact. That's the win. A late laugh at a joke is not pity. A tired person is a tired person. I have a list now. It's shorter than the funeral list and much more annoying to the scanner, which is how I know it's true. Staying happened. Several times. I am not a dress rehearsal for being left. I am a friend who sometimes panics and still gets to be picked up on Thursday.

REJECTION: One Thursday isn't a pattern.

LIAM: Neither is one pause. You don't get to be the only one allowed a sample size. Sit down. Thursday is the pattern until you bring better data.

---

**THE YAWN**

ADAM: Walk me through the yawn like it's a court transcript. I want the scanner cross-examined in front of the class.

LIAM: You yawned at 9:40. I decided, in under a second, that the friendship had peaked and you were being polite. I drafted a goodbye in my head that was dignified and insane. I smiled too much. I offered to leave early so you wouldn't have to do the hard part. You said, I'm just tired, sit down, and ordered chips. The chips were the second sentence. I almost didn't hear them over the funeral.

REJECTION: The yawn was data.

LIAM: The yawn was a mouth. The chips were data. You archived the wrong exhibit. If this were a real court they'd throw out your case and bill you for the chips.

ADAM: What should I say when I see you start the funeral. Give me a line that isn't therapy speak.

LIAM: "I'm not leaving, I'm tired, pass the sauce." That's the line. If you add "don't be sensitive" you've joined the scanner. If you give a speech about your commitment, I start looking for the exit in the speech. Sauce. Tired. Still here. Three words would do. I'm being generous with a full sentence.

REJECTION: A sentence can still be a lie.

LIAM: So can a silence. You treat silence as honest and speech as suspicious. That's not pattern recognition. That's a mood with a law degree.

ADAM: The almost-ended things. One you actually walked back. How.

LIAM: I sent the draft. Then I sent a second text that said ignore that, I did the thing, I'm still in. They replied "I know, sauce is in the fridge, come over." I went over. I did not deliver the closing argument. The scanner called it a stay of execution. I call it Wednesday. Wednesday is underrated. It doesn't get a soundtrack. It gets a fridge.

REJECTION: They'll tire of the second text.

LIAM: Then they'll tell me, in words, and I'll survive words. What I won't do is resign from a job I still have because a yawn looked like a letter of termination. I've resigned from enough phantom jobs. I'm keeping this one. Sauce in the fridge. That's the contract. Initial it.

REJECTION: I don't initial things that make us soft.

LIAM: Then don't. I'll initial it for both of us. Soft is how people stay. Hard is how I eat chips alone and call it insight.

---

ADAM: Afterward. Liam's mind moves quickly. The sensitivity moves faster. The people who stay have learned to slow the translation. Next time. Sophie. And the hostile witness.
