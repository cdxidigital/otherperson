# EPISODE 6 — THE EX WHO STILL HAS A KEY
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Tom
**Third voice:** Addiction
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Park bench. No using detail. No methods. The charm, the lease, the boring Tuesdays. Argue with the ex. Don't glamorise it.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Alcohol. Addiction. Recovery. No play-by-play. No instructions. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode six. Tom.

TOM: You picked a bench with a view of a pub. Bold.

ADAM: I picked a bench with a view of a tree. The pub is the tree's problem.

TOM: The pub is always someone's problem. Sit. Empty space beside me still feels like it has a key. That's the guest.

ADAM: Is it here.

TOM: It's always here. It just doesn't always speak. Today it will, because you brought a microphone and it loves an audience.

---

**THE EX**

ADAM: Introduce it.

TOM: An ex who still has a key and tries the door like nothing happened. Charismatic. Remembers every good night. None of the damage. Excellent timing. Terrible housemate.

ADDICTION: I remember the good nights because they were good. You're welcome for the archive.

TOM: You remember them with the lights up and the ending deleted. That's not an archive. That's a trailer.

ADDICTION: Trailers sell the film. You used to like the film.

TOM: I liked the first twenty minutes. Then you rewrote the lease from the inside and changed the locks and called it a relationship.

ADAM: How did it start. Keep it boring on purpose.

TOM: Fun. That's the annoying truth. It made things easier, louder, more possible. I was funnier. I was less scared. Then the terms changed and I kept telling myself the next time would be different. This time I'd be in charge. I never was. I just stopped reading the fine print because the fine print ruined the bit.

ADDICTION: I solved the feeling when nothing else showed up on time. Immediate. Reliable. Temporary. He calls that a trick. I call it customer service.

TOM: Customer service doesn't move into the house and tell your friends they're boring.

ADDICTION: Your friends were boring. They wanted to talk about feelings. I wanted to solve them.

ADAM: You turned people into an audience or an obstacle.

TOM: Yeah. That's the bit I hate saying out loud because it makes me sound like a villain in a story I also suffered inside. Both can be true. I was harmed and I did harm. The ex loves it when I only tell one half.

ADDICTION: The half where you're the victim plays better. The half where you were cruel is bad marketing.

TOM: I'm not marketing. I'm on a bench.

---

**THE TUESDAYS**

ADAM: People want the speech. The anniversary. The before-and-after.

TOM: People want a montage. Recovery is not a montage. It is a thousand small, deeply unsexy decisions. The myth of the one big night is sold to people who haven't done a flat Tuesday yet. Celebrate the boring parts. They are the ones that keep the door locked.

ADDICTION: Boring is a phase. I can wait. I am extremely patient. And I have excellent timing.

TOM: I know. That's your whole threat. You don't kick the door down. You wait until I'm tired, or proud, or at a wedding, and you try the handle like an old joke.

ADAM: Has it tried recently.

TOM: Last month. Not dramatically. A thought turned up dressed as a good idea, like it had ironed a shirt. I recognised the footsteps and I didn't open the door. That's the story. No montage. No slow clap. I made a sandwich and stayed in my own life, which is frankly a bit rude of me, given the marketing budget.

ADDICTION: One locked door is not a personality. He'll get bored of being sober and sensible.

TOM: I am already bored. I do it anyway. Boredom is not a reason to let you back in. It's a reason to get a hobby that isn't you.

ADAM: Pottery.

TOM: Don't start. Marcus already suffered.

---

**WHAT PEOPLE MISS**

ADAM: What do you wish people understood.

TOM: That the work is on the quiet weeks, not the collapse. If you only show up when I'm falling, you miss the actual job. Come over on a flat Tuesday. Don't make the flatness mean I'm failing. Don't make a joke that hands the ex the microphone. And don't ask me to entertain you with the old version. That version had better stories and a worse ending.

ADDICTION: The old stories were better. He knows it. Look at his face.

TOM: My face is allowed to miss a feeling without offering you a key. Missing is not consent.

ADAM: Say the contract.

TOM: You can knock. I don't have to answer. You don't get to rewrite the good nights into proof that the bad ones didn't count. You don't get my friends, my Tuesdays, or my boredom. The key you think you have is a story. The lock is real. I changed it. You just haven't updated your trailer.

ADDICTION: I'll keep walking the hallway.

TOM: Walk it. I'm making dinner. You can smell it. You're not getting a plate. The rebellion is a chicken thigh and a locked door. If that's embarrassing, good. Embarrassment means I'm still the one holding the spatula.

ADAM: I nearly laughed then in the wrong place. Like the chicken was the bit and you were the setup.

TOM: Laugh at the ex. It's a ridiculous tenant. Don't laugh like I was stupid for letting it have a key. I was tired, and it was charming, and charming is a hell of a sales pitch when you're twenty-four. I'm not stupid. I'm the bloke who changed the lock and still cooks. Both can be funny. Only one of them gets to be the joke.

ADAM: The friends you lost. Not the montage. The ordinary damage.

TOM: I became a person who promised and didn't arrive, or arrived and wasn't really there. I made people manage my mood so the night could continue. Some of them left. They were right to. I don't get to call that betrayal. A couple stayed and I didn't know what to do with them, because staying didn't fit the story that everyone would give up. The ex prefers the giving-up story. It's cleaner marketing.

ADDICTION: Staying is how people get bored and start making rules.

TOM: Staying is how I found out I could be loved without performing the fun version. You hated that discovery. You still do.

ADAM: What does a locked door sound like on a normal night. No drama.

TOM: The thought arrives dressed as a good idea. I recognise the coat. I eat something. I text someone who knows the deal and I don't make them counsel me for an hour. I go to bed at a boring time. In the morning it's still a life. That's the whole sound. A kettle. Not a speech.

ADDICTION: Speeches are temporary. I outlast speeches.

TOM: You don't outlast dinners and early nights. You just talk like you do. I'm done being impressed by your patience. Patience without a key is just loitering.

ADAM: What should we celebrate, if we're not going to be those people who only turn up for the collapse.

TOM: The flat week. The plan I kept. The joke that wasn't a deflection. The fact I can sit on this bench and let you mention a pub without turning it into a test I have to pass out loud. Celebrate the absence of an event. It's bad content. It's good friendship.

ADDICTION: Bad content doesn't trend.

TOM: I'm not trying to trend. I'm trying to still know my friends in five years. Get off my bench.

ADAM: Say the thing you wish someone had said in the first year, before it became a trailer you starred in.

TOM: I wish someone had said the fun wasn't proof the terms were fair. I wish they'd said you can miss a feeling and still refuse the invoice. I wish they'd sat on a flat Tuesday before I was interesting enough to rescue. Rescue is a spectacle. Company is a practice. I needed the practice.

ADDICTION: Company doesn't fix the feeling as fast as I do.

TOM: Company doesn't invoice me in friendships. You do. Speed isn't care. It's a sales tactic. I'm not buying.

ADAM: And now. What do you say to the version of you who still thinks the next time would be different.

TOM: It wouldn't. You already read the ending. You just liked the lighting. Come home. Eat. Lock the door. Let the hallway creak. You don't owe it a conversation. You owe the people who stayed a Tuesday. Start there. It's smaller than you think and larger than the trailer.

ADDICTION: I'll still be in the hallway tomorrow.

TOM: Bring a coat. You're not coming in. We've had this talk. I'm bored of your timing. It's not charming anymore. It's a knock-off.

ADAM: Last one. The friends who only know the trailer. What do they need to hear so they don't keep casting you in it.

TOM: I'm not funnier when I'm gone. I'm smaller. If you miss the loud version, miss it quietly and still invite the quiet one. Don't hand me a drink as a joke. Don't tell the old story like it's a compliment. The old story has a cost that doesn't fit in a punchline. Come to the flat Tuesday or admit you wanted the character, not the friend.

ADDICTION: The character was loved.

TOM: The character was applauded. There's a difference. I'm done auditioning for it. The door stays locked. The kettle goes on. That's the scene. No trailer.

TOM: And if you only have one minute with me, don't spend it on the trailer. Ask what I ate. Ask if the door stayed shut. If I say yes, that's the plot. You don't need the deleted scenes. The deleted scenes are how the ex gets back into the conversation. Leave them deleted.

ADDICTION: Deleted scenes are the best scenes.

TOM: They're deleted because they cost people. I'm keeping the kettle. You can keep the nostalgia. It doesn't open anything.

---

**TUESDAY, SPECIFICALLY**

ADAM: Describe the flat Tuesday. Not the slogan. The hour. I want the ex to hear how unmarketable it is.

TOM: Six. I eat something that isn't a story. I wash the plate. I text my sister back about nothing, a photo of a dog, a time for lunch. I walk the same block. The hallway creaks and I do not open a conversation with it. I watch half a show and I don't turn the half into a feeling that needs solving. I sleep. That's the plot. If you're waiting for the montage, you'll miss it, because the montage is the point and it looks like dishes.

ADDICTION: Dishes are what people do when they've given up.

TOM: Dishes are what people do when they still live here. You've confused a life with a low point. Classic sales move.

ADAM: The friend who still tells the old story at dinner. What do you want them to do with their mouth.

TOM: Retire it. Not with a speech about respect. Just stop. If they need a story, tell the one where I turned up to the barbecue sober and remembered people's kids' names. It's less funny. It's the one that didn't cost anyone a lift home at 2 a.m. If they can't love that story, they loved the chaos, and the chaos has left the group chat.

ADDICTION: They laughed harder then.

TOM: They laughed because they weren't the one cleaning it up. I'm done being the entertainment budget.

ADAM: Cravings. You don't have to perform one. What do you want from a person in the room when the knock happens and you don't answer.

TOM: Don't hover. Don't search my face for a plot twist. Sit in the kitchen. Talk about the dog. If I say the knock happened, say good, you didn't answer, and then pass the tea. If I say nothing, don't interview me. The interview makes the ex feel famous. The tea makes the Tuesday real. Famous is how it gets back in. Real is how it stays in the hall.

ADDICTION: Famous worked for years.

TOM: Famous emptied the room. I'm keeping the ones who stayed for the dishes. You can have the applause. It's recorded. It doesn't knock.

---

ADAM: Afterward. Tom's recovery is quiet and daily. The people who only clap for the dramatic version miss the work. Next time. Aisha. And the national emergency that starts in a supermarket aisle.
