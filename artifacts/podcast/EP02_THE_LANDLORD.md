# EPISODE 2 — THE LANDLORD
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Marcus
**Third voice:** Depression
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Blinds finally open. Two coffees actually being drunk. Interrupt. Argue. Comedy first, then the weight.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Depression. Numbness. Going quiet. The cover story of tired. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode two. Marcus.

MARCUS: You opened the blinds.

ADAM: You said the room looks like a storage unit.

MARCUS: It is a storage unit. I live in it. There's a difference of about one pillow.

ADAM: Two coffees. Both actually being drunk. That's already a better episode than last time I was here.

MARCUS: Last time you talked to the kettle.

ADAM: The kettle was the only one answering.

MARCUS: Rude. Accurate. Don't get cocky.

ADAM: Empty chair opposite you. It already looks like it pays the rent.

MARCUS: It does. I'm the difficult tenant.

---

**INTRODUCE THE HOUSEMATE**

ADAM: Introduce your housemate.

MARCUS: Tired. Older than me. Sits very still. Not cruel. Just deeply convinced that rest is the only honest response to being alive. Looks at me like a landlord who knows I can't afford to move out. Given up on renovations. Just wants everyone to stop asking for things.

DEPRESSION: I can hear the renovations comment.

MARCUS: Good. The kitchen tap has dripped since 2019.

DEPRESSION: And yet you are still here. That's called stability.

MARCUS: That's called I lost the bond.

ADAM: It talks like it owns the place.

DEPRESSION: I do own the place. He thinks he is subletting his own life. Very modern arrangement.

MARCUS: See. Landlord. I told you.

DEPRESSION: You also told him you were tired. Which was my line. Stop stealing my material.

---

**THE KITCHEN**

ADAM: When did it move in.

MARCUS: Sixteen. Kitchen. Dad had just left. Front door closed and the house went quiet in a way that felt structural. Like someone had pulled a load-bearing wall out and expected the roof to negotiate.

ADAM: What did you feel.

MARCUS: That's the joke. I waited for the film version. Sadness. Rage. A speech. Something I could point at and say there, that's the feeling, please take it outside. Instead there was thick heavy nothing. Concrete poured into the space where emotions were supposed to live. I thought it was shock. Shock is meant to pass. This one signed a lease.

DEPRESSION: Shock leaves. I stay. Someone has to look after the property.

MARCUS: You don't look after it. You sit in it.

DEPRESSION: Sitting is a form of care. Very underrated.

ADAM: What did you tell people.

MARCUS: Tired. For twenty years. Tired is the Swiss Army knife of cover stories. It lets people stop asking without making them feel like bad friends. I used it after fourteen hours of sleep. I used it at lunch. I used it at a wedding while holding a drink I wasn't finishing.

ADAM: I believed you.

MARCUS: I know. That's why it worked. You're not thick. The story was just very good.

DEPRESSION: The story was excellent. Minimal admin. High compliance.

MARCUS: Don't compliment my lies. They're your lies. I was just the spokesperson.

---

**POTTERY**

ADAM: First time you tried to say it properly.

MARCUS: Early twenties. Girlfriend. She asked why I'd gone quiet for three weeks. I tried to explain the nothing. She said maybe I needed more hobbies.

ADAM: Hobbies.

MARCUS: More hobbies. Right. Because the problem was insufficient pottery. I almost laughed. Then I didn't, because laughing would have required a mood, and the mood was out getting milk and never came back.

DEPRESSION: Pottery would not have helped. I want that on the record.

MARCUS: Nothing was going to help that week. That's not the same as nothing ever helps. You collapse those two sentences on purpose.

DEPRESSION: Efficiency.

ADAM: So you stopped explaining.

MARCUS: Easier to be the reliable bloke who sometimes goes dark than to keep failing at language. People can forgive a ghost. They get impatient with a philosopher.

ADAM: I invented reasons for you. Work. Busy. Early start.

MARCUS: I know. I let you. That was a joint venture. Don't take all the blame and don't take none of it.

---

**WHAT IT SAYS**

ADAM: When it's strong, what does it actually say. Not the brochure. The 3 a.m. version.

MARCUS: It doesn't shout. That's the scam. Shouting I could argue with. It waits until I agree. Effort is pointless. You're already behind. People would be better off if you stayed still and stopped needing things. It's very polite about the demolition. That's the worst part.

DEPRESSION: Polite demolition has fewer complaints.

MARCUS: From who.

DEPRESSION: From the neighbours. From him. From you, Adam, who prefers a quiet exit to a scene.

ADAM: Don't bring me into your tenancy dispute.

DEPRESSION: You already live in it. You just visit.

ADAM: Chair. What do you want from him.

DEPRESSION: Rest. Permanent rest. Stop performing competence. Stop hoping tomorrow will be different. The hope is frankly embarrassing for both of us.

MARCUS: Hear that. It thinks hope is embarrassing. Like I've turned up to a funeral in a party hat.

DEPRESSION: You have. Several times. I have the photos.

MARCUS: Those were birthdays.

DEPRESSION: Same lighting.

ADAM: Do you ever find the explanation comforting.

MARCUS: I hate that the answer is sometimes yes. Without it, the emptiness feels personal. With it, the emptiness has a landlord and a reason. Both things can be true. That's the most annoying sentence in the entire arrangement.

DEPRESSION: I'm almost merciful. In a completely unhelpful way. That's my brand.

MARCUS: Put it on a tea towel.

DEPRESSION: I don't do merch. I do mornings.

---

**A SPECIFIC NIGHT**

ADAM: Forty minutes. That's the number I remember. You used to stay till the last bottle. Then you started leaving at forty minutes and we all pretended it was traffic.

MARCUS: It was never traffic. Forty minutes is how long I can perform a person before the rent comes due. After that the landlord wants the lounge back.

ADAM: Whose birthday was it. Be honest.

MARCUS: Yours. Three years ago. You thought I had an early job. I sat in the car for twenty minutes because going back in felt like a lie and driving home felt like a confession. I chose the confession and didn't tell anyone.

ADAM: I would have wanted you in the car park. Not the performance.

MARCUS: I know that now. I didn't know it then. Then, staying quiet felt like protecting you from the weather in my head.

DEPRESSION: And it worked. He had a nice party.

MARCUS: He had a nice party with a missing chair and a story about traffic. That's not the same as fine.

ADAM: What does the nothing feel like in the body. You don't have to make it pretty.

MARCUS: Heavy. Not sad-heavy. Furniture-heavy. Like someone moved a couch onto my chest and then went out. Arms work. Legs work. The bit that starts things doesn't. Caring feels like lifting something I cannot currently lift. That is not the same as not caring.

DEPRESSION: He cares. It's inefficient. I keep suggesting he stop.

MARCUS: And I keep not stopping. That's the part you leave off the invoice.

---

**THE CONTRACT**

ADAM: Do you want it gone.

MARCUS: I want the weight lifted. I also don't know who I am without the explanation. If you evict the landlord, what do I tell people I was doing for twenty years. Sleeping. Being difficult. Becoming interesting. None of those are true and all of them have been useful.

DEPRESSION: Without me he would have to believe the emptiness is personal. This way it has paperwork. You're welcome.

MARCUS: I didn't ask for paperwork. I asked for a Tuesday that doesn't feel like wading.

ADAM: New contract. Say it to the chair, not to me.

MARCUS: You can sit in the room. You cannot lock the door from the inside. You can call it rest. You cannot call my friends a waste of effort. You don't get to leave early and then tell me I left. And you don't get to use the word tired. I retired that word. It's yours if you want it. I'm done being your spokesperson.

DEPRESSION: I'll take the word. I always liked it.

MARCUS: Of course you did. It never had to lift anything.

ADAM: What should the rest of us do when you go quiet.

MARCUS: Stay anyway. Don't invent pottery. If you turn up with clay I will use it as a doorstop and you will deserve it. Don't track me like a renovation. "You seem better" is just applause for a blind. Ask if I want the good mug. If I say yes, that's the news. If I say nothing, pour it. You don't have to clap. Clapping is how this turns into a telethon.

DEPRESSION: He will still go quiet.

MARCUS: And they can still stay. Those are allowed to happen in the same week.

ADAM: One more thing before I let the landlord have the last smug line. What does a good day actually look like. Not a cured day. A possible one.

MARCUS: A coffee that tastes like coffee. Answering a text the same day. Not performing a personality for the first hour. Opening the blinds without treating it as a press release. If I do all four, the landlord still sends an invoice, but the invoice is smaller.

DEPRESSION: Smaller invoices are still invoices. Don't get sentimental.

MARCUS: I'm allowed to be sentimental about a Tuesday. You don't own Tuesdays. You rent the mornings.

ADAM: And the people who love you. What do we stop doing.

MARCUS: Stop the progress report. "You seem better" is a trap with a smile on it. If I say yes, I've jinxed the afternoon. If I say no, I've disappointed a man who opened my blinds like he was liberating a hostage. Ask what I want for dinner. If I have an answer, eat it. If I don't, pick anyway and don't narrate the picking. That's not therapy. That's chips. I can do chips.

DEPRESSION: Dinner is a distraction.

MARCUS: Dinner is a life. You've been very committed to missing the point.

---

**THE GREY TICK**

ADAM: The group chat. Be honest. What happens when everyone is making plans and you are the grey tick.

MARCUS: The grey tick is a personality now. I open it. I read the jokes. I type three replies and delete them because they sound like a person I am not currently employing. Then the landlord says, see, you don't belong in the bright room. I put the phone face down and call that a decision.

DEPRESSION: Face down is rest.

MARCUS: Face down is hiding. Rest would be a nap and a text that says I'm out tonight, love you. I can do the nap. The text is apparently an extreme sport.

ADAM: Send one while I'm here. Not a redemption scene. A Tuesday. I'll look at the kettle.

MARCUS: Fine. Can't tonight. Not personal. Have one for me. There. The landlord hated every word.

DEPRESSION: I hated tonight. It implies other nights. Optimistic. Reckless.

MARCUS: There will be other nights. Some of them I'll attend. You don't get to pre-grieve my calendar.

ADAM: What do we do with the grey tick from our side. And if you say pottery I am leaving.

MARCUS: Don't narrate my absence to the group. Marcus is being weird becomes a story I then have to re-enter. Say he's out, we'll see him. If I come back, don't do a welcome parade. A parade means I was gone in a way that needs forgiving. I needed a chair. That's different.

DEPRESSION: He was absent. Call it what it is.

MARCUS: I was absent from the pub. I was not absent from my life. You love collapsing those.

ADAM: One thing you still want that the landlord calls embarrassing.

MARCUS: To be invited even when I say no. The invitation is the proof the chair still exists. If people stop asking, the landlord gets to say I told you they were better off. Keep asking. Let me decline without a tribunal. That's the whole friendship. Ask. Allow no. Leave the chair.

DEPRESSION: They will get bored of asking.

MARCUS: Some will. The ones who don't are the house. You can sneer at them from the hallway. You don't get their keys.

ADAM: And if I am the one who stopped asking for a year.

MARCUS: Then start again without a speech about how you've grown. A message that says pub Thursday, no is fine, is worth more than an apology essay. I don't need you punished. I need the chair put back.

---

ADAM: Afterward. I invented normal reasons for Marcus for years. Work. Tired. Busy. The silence after I asked what the nothing felt like was the most honest answer he gave me. The landlord is still in the chair. Marcus is still in the house. Next time. Elena. And the security guard who never clocks off.
