# EPISODE 7 — NATIONAL EMERGENCY
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Guest:** Aisha
**Third voice:** Panic
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** Door visible. Comedy about a body that calls a committee. No instructions. Presence without matching the alarm.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. Panic. False alarms that feel real. Bodies that don't consult the person living in them. I'm Adam James. This is The Other Person in the Room. Episode seven. Aisha.

AISHA: You can see the door. Good. If you couldn't, this would be a different episode and a shorter friendship.

ADAM: I clocked the exits on the way in. I'm becoming Elena.

AISHA: Don't. One security guard in the group is enough. Mine doesn't scan. Mine evacuates.

ADAM: Introduce it before it declares a state of emergency about the mic.

---

**THE ALARM**

AISHA: Loud. Certain. Completely uninterested in proportion. Treats a slightly raised heart rate like the building is on fire and the exits are blocked by bees.

PANIC: The bees are a metaphor. The fire is not. Move.

AISHA: We are sitting. There is no fire. There is a man with a microphone and a biscuit.

PANIC: Biscuits are how it starts. People get comfortable. Then the heart does a small thing. Then I am needed.

AISHA: You are needed the way a car alarm is needed in an empty street at 3 a.m. Technically employed. Socially a menace.

ADAM: What does it feel like when it arrives. Keep it human.

AISHA: A national emergency declared by a body that did not consult the government. A supermarket aisle can become a disaster film in under ten seconds. The lights are too bright. The floor moves. My heart files a report that says we are dying, and it files it with total confidence. The previous fifty times it was wrong do not get a vote.

PANIC: This time is different.

AISHA: You always say that.

PANIC: Because this time is different. The old data is irrelevant. I do not do maybe later. I do not do let's assess. I do full evacuation and I do it with confidence.

ADAM: And afterwards.

AISHA: Shame. Which is rude, because I didn't book the emergency. I just had to leave a trolley in an aisle like a person who changed her mind about yoghurt. People stare. The alarm does not stay to explain.

PANIC: Explanation is not my department. Exit is.

---

**DON'T SAY CALM DOWN**

ADAM: What is the least useful sentence in the English language.

AISHA: Calm down. Close second: it's all in your head. Third: just breathe, said by someone who is breathing normally and feels like a genius about it. My head is not a separate country from my body. That's the whole problem. They are in a group chat and panic is the admin.

ADAM: I have said calm down.

AISHA: You have. You said it outside a cinema. I wanted to file you in the bin. I also wanted you to stay. Both. The useful version of you was the one who walked me to the car and didn't narrate it.

ADAM: What does useful look like.

AISHA: Smaller room. Quieter room. A door I can see. You nearby without catching the alarm. Don't match my volume. Don't demand a speech while my body is still evacuating. Wait. The false alarm finishes its shift. It always does. It just refuses to be rushed.

PANIC: I do not clock out early. That is not how emergencies work.

AISHA: It is not an emergency. It is a dress rehearsal your body forgot was fake.

PANIC: Dress rehearsals save lives.

AISHA: They also ruin a Tuesday shop. We contain multitudes. Unfortunately.

---

**THE NAME AND THE CONTRACT**

ADAM: When did you stop thinking you were dying and start thinking you were being lied to by a very committed system.

AISHA: Not cleanly. Years. A doctor gave it a name and I was offended and relieved. Offended because it felt like being told my disaster movie was a false alarm. Relieved because false alarms are a category, and categories mean I'm not uniquely broken. I still have to wait the alarm out. The name doesn't switch it off. It just stops me adding a second catastrophe called what is wrong with me.

ADAM: Do you want it gone.

AISHA: I want proportion. I want a heart that can jog, or see a bright aisle, or drink a coffee, without calling a national cabinet. I don't need a body that never warns me. I need a body that can be wrong in a smaller font.

PANIC: Smaller font gets people killed.

AISHA: Larger font gets me standing in car parks apologising to people I love. We're negotiating font size. That's the relationship.

ADAM: Say the contract.

AISHA: You can warn me. You cannot evacuate my whole day because my heart did a cartwheel. You don't get to call every time "this time." You don't get my friends as an audience for the debrief. And you don't get to be offended when I stay in the room anyway. Staying is not me ignoring you. Staying is me outranking you.

PANIC: I will finish when I finish. Your schedule is not my problem.

AISHA: My schedule is the point of having a life. Get a hobby that isn't my pulse.

ADAM: Tell me about the cinema properly. I was there. I got it wrong. Let's do the tape.

AISHA: The film was loud. My heart matched it. Panic decided the matching was a medical event and not a speaker system. I stood up. You said calm down, quietly, like volume was the issue. It wasn't. I needed the foyer, a wall behind me, and you to be a person and not a narrator. You got there eventually. The eventually cost me a scene in a corridor I didn't want to star in.

PANIC: The corridor was correct. The film was a threat.

AISHA: The film was a film. You have no notes on cinema. Stay in your lane, which is unfortunately my ribcage.

ADAM: What do you wish you'd been able to say in the foyer.

AISHA: I'm not dying. Don't make me convince you of that while it's happening. Just come with me and be bored. Boredom is the gift. If you look frightened, I have to manage your fright as well as mine, and I am already fully staffed by this idiot.

PANIC: Idiot who has kept you alive.

AISHA: Idiot who has kept me leaving rooms. We can both be on the poster.

ADAM: A good ordinary week. What does it contain.

AISHA: Shops I finish. A coffee I don't abandon. A heart that does something weird and a mind that says we've seen this film. I still get the spike. The spike just doesn't get the rest of the afternoon as often. That's not a cure. That's a truce with better borders.

PANIC: Truces expire.

AISHA: So do your emergencies. I've watched them expire in car parks all over this city. You're reliable in one way only. You end. I wish you'd lead with that.

ADAM: The apology you give afterwards. Let's argue with that too.

AISHA: I apologise for leaving, for the trolley, for the look on people's faces, for needing the foyer. The alarm loves the apology. It turns a false emergency into a moral failure, so next time I hesitate to leave when leaving would actually help. The apology is a second shift. I didn't apply for it.

PANIC: Apologies keep the group stable.

AISHA: The group is stable if I come back and nobody makes me perform guilt. Watch. Adam. I left the cinema. I'm not sorry I left. I'm sorry the film was loud and you were a bit useless for ninety seconds. That's the accurate apology. Smaller. True.

ADAM: Accepted. I was a bit useless. Then I wasn't.

AISHA: That's the whole friendship. Useless, then present. Don't skip to the speech about how well I handled it. I handled it by surviving a liar in my chest. You handled it by walking. Neither of us needs a medal. We need the next ordinary shop to be finishable.

PANIC: The next one will be different.

AISHA: You are a broken record and I am turning the volume down. Not off. Down. Live with it.

ADAM: For the person listening who loves an Aisha and freezes. One instruction. Then we stop.

AISHA: Don't fix the body. Move the body to a smaller room if I ask. Stay boring. Boring is regulating. Match my feet, not my fear. When it passes, don't hold a review. Ask if I want a lift, a quiet, or a joke. Then do that thing and no extra thing. The extra thing is usually calm down in a nicer coat.

PANIC: If they stay boring, they miss the emergency.

AISHA: If they stay boring, they miss your audition. Good. The emergency was never theirs to star in. It was a false alarm with good lighting. I'm keeping the friends. You can keep the lighting. We're done for today.

AISHA: And if it happens in your kitchen, not a cinema. Same rules. Don't narrate. Don't block the door. Don't make a face like you've discovered a medical mystery. Put the kettle on if I want it. Sit where I can see the exit. Let the alarm lie until it gets bored. Boredom is the cure I can actually use. Your panic on my behalf is just a second alarm. One is enough.

PANIC: Boredom is dangerous.

AISHA: Boredom is Tuesday. I'm taking Tuesday back. You've had the building long enough.

---

**THE TROLLEY**

ADAM: Tell the trolley story properly. Not the highlight. The ugly funny middle, then the cost. I can hold both.

AISHA: I'm in the aisle, comparing oats like a person, and then my heart files a report that says we are dying and also everyone is watching. I abandon a perfectly good trolley like it's a crime scene. A stranger asks if I need a manager. I need a bench and a less ambitious cardiovascular system. I sit on the floor by the flowers, which is not dignified, and I wait the liar out. Twelve minutes. The oats are still in the trolley. I go back and buy them. That's the heroic bit nobody claps for. Returning to oats.

PANIC: Twelve minutes is a lifetime. I was right to clear the building.

AISHA: You cleared a building that was a supermarket. The only casualty was a metaphor and my dignity. The oats survived. So did I. Your report was fan fiction.

ADAM: What do I do if I find you on the floor by the flowers. Wrong answers first, so we can laugh, then the real one.

AISHA: Wrong: calm down, have you tried breathing, why is this happening, should I call someone, you were fine a minute ago, people are looking. Right: hi, I'm here, floor's available, do you want me down here or standing where you can see me. Then you shut up until I pick. If I pick oats, we buy oats. If I pick outside, we go outside. You don't add a third option called processing.

PANIC: Processing prevents the next one.

AISHA: Processing in the flower aisle prevents the oats. The next one will come anyway. You are very committed to being early and wrong.

ADAM: The apology you used to text me after. We're deleting the draft. What replaces it.

AISHA: "Left the shop. I'm okay. See you at yours." No sorry. No essay. If you need more, you can ask later, once my ribs have stopped doing percussion. The essay is the alarm's sequel. I don't do sequels on the same day.

PANIC: They'll think you're rude.

AISHA: They'll think I survived a liar and still bought dinner. That's not rude. That's a better plot than yours.

---

ADAM: Afterward. Aisha has left rooms that looked ordinary from the outside. They were not ordinary to her nervous system. Next time. Liam. And advanced pattern recognition.
