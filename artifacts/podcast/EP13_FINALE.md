# EPISODE 13 — THE OTHER PERSON WAS ME
**Show:** The Other Person in the Room
**Host:** Adam James
**Runtime target:** 12–15 minutes
**Tone:** No guest in the chair at first. Then the season answers back in short lines. Adam gets interrogated by the rooms he skipped. Funny, then he stops hiding. Not a speech. A confession with interruptions.

---

**CONTENT NOTE**

ADAM: Content note. This last one gathers the season. Same subjects. Same care. If a room was too much the first time, you can leave this one too. I'm Adam James. This is the last episode of The Other Person in the Room.

---

**THE EMPTY CHAIR**

ADAM: I have spent twelve episodes pointing at other people's empty chairs. Very brave of me. Very journalistic. The chair in this room is mine and I have been introducing it as "production."

FEAR: It's not production.

ADAM: Oh good. You're early.

FEAR: I'm always early. You call it preparation. I call it not letting you say the true thing first.

ADAM: The true thing is I thought this show was about my friends. It was. It also turned into an inventory of the ways I leave rooms while still sitting in them. Cancelled plans I blamed on work. The performance of fine. The jokes I make so nobody asks a second question.

SHAME: The jokes are mine.

ADAM: There are two of you. Fantastic. A panel.

SHAME: Fear stops you starting. I stop you staying once you've started. We're a small business. We don't need a third partner. You keep applying.

ADAM: I interviewed Sarah and teased the stove for years. I interviewed Marcus and invented traffic. I stood behind Elena. I said calm down to Aisha. I asked Jordan if he was better. I thought Mei was cold. If this show had a blooper reel it would just be my face.

FEAR: And yet you want credit for noticing later. Later is still you arriving after the damage and asking for a biscuit.

ADAM: I'm not asking for a biscuit. I'm asking to say it without you editing the sentence into something nicer.

SHAME: Nicer plays better.

ADAM: Nicer is how I lost ten years of telling the truth in kitchens.

---

**WHAT HE HID**

ADAM: Here's the bit I kept cutting out of my own script. I have a room too. Not a diagnosis I'm going to borrow from my friends to sound profound. A plain one. Fear of being seen as the man who needs something. Shame about needing it. I can organise everyone else's chair and forget I own one.

FEAR: If you need something, they leave.

ADAM: That's your theory. The evidence from this season is the opposite. They stayed when the need was visible. They struggled when it was disguised as fine.

SHAME: Fine is polite.

ADAM: Fine is a locked door with a welcome mat. I have used it on Sarah. On Marcus. On dates. On my own family. I would rather be misunderstood than be a burden, and then I resent people for misunderstanding me. That's not mystery. That's a con.

SHAME: You did the con because the alternative was pathetic.

ADAM: The alternative was specific. "I'm not okay, and I'm still coming." I watched twelve people try versions of that sentence. I clapped from the safe side of the mic.

FEAR: The mic is safer. Keep the mic.

ADAM: The mic is a hiding place with better lighting.

---

**THE CALLBACKS**

ADAM: I'm going to read what they asked for. Not as inspiration. As a list of instructions I ignored in my own house.

SARAH: Fewer pages on the clipboard. If I laugh, that's not an emergency.

ADAM: I treat my own good mood as suspicious. Like if I enjoy a day, the bill is coming. That's her clipboard in my voice.

MARCUS: Stay anyway. Don't invent pottery.

ADAM: I invent busy. Busy is my pottery.

ELENA: Don't stand behind me. I'm still here. Write it down.

ADAM: I don't write down my own still-here. I write down the near misses and call it realism.

DAVID: Make room for the weather. Don't co-sign the 1 a.m. plan.

ADAM: I co-sign my own panic plans at midnight and then shame myself in the morning. I am both of his people and neither of them likes me.

PRIYA: One pass. Not a trilogy. Lateness is not my personality.

ADAM: I rehearse texts until the friendship has moved on. That's a play. I directed it.

TOM: Come over on the flat Tuesday. Don't clap only for the collapse.

ADAM: I am dramatic about my own bad weeks and silent about the ordinary ones. The ordinary ones are the work. I wanted a montage.

AISHA: Don't say calm down. Stay without matching the alarm.

ADAM: I say calm down to myself in a mirror like that's a treatment. It isn't. It's heckling.

LIAM: A second sentence. This is not the ending.

ADAM: I write the ending first so I can be right about being left. If I leave first, I don't have to feel it.

SOPHIE: Don't join the witness. Talk about the day.

ADAM: I narrate my own face, my own work, my own worth, like a hostile expert. My friends would never speak to me the way I speak to me. I would stop them.

JORDAN: No graph. Say the name. Sit on your side of the bench.

ADAM: I want a graph for sadness. I want it to peak and resolve before the weekend. It doesn't. I have been rude to time.

MEI: The quiet is the sound of staying. Small is not nothing.

ADAM: I fill silence because I think silence means I've failed as a host. Sometimes silence means the other person is spending everything they have to remain. I should move the table and shut up.

---

**THE CONTRACT WITH HIMSELF**

FEAR: This is the part where you promise to be better and then call us in the morning.

ADAM: This is the part where I stop promising a personality transplant. You can warn me. You cannot veto the sentence. Shame, you can notice a real harm I've done. You cannot call needing help a harm.

SHAME: Needing help is ugly.

ADAM: Needing help is how every person on this show stayed in the room. I don't get a special exemption because I hold the microphone.

FEAR: If you say it, they'll hear you.

ADAM: Good. They've been hearing a polished version. The polished version is why I missed the stove checks and the forty-minute exits and the flinch. I'm tired of being the last to understand my own friends because I was busy sounding all right.

SHAME: And if they think less of you.

ADAM: Then they think less of me. That's data. It's not a death. Liam would tell me to wait twenty minutes before I publish the tragedy. I'm waiting.

---

**LAST QUESTION**

ADAM: I asked each of them the same question. If you could introduce the person you are now to the person you were before this began, what would you say. I owe myself that. So. Adam at the start, who thought love could out-argue a nervous system. Who teased, invented traffic, stood in the wrong place, and called it friendship.

I'd say: you don't get to narrate people you haven't sat with. Sit down. Leave the silences. The other person in your room is not a brand. It's a habit of leaving before anyone can leave you. You can fire the habit. You don't fire yourself.

FEAR: We'll still be here tomorrow.

ADAM: Bring a coat. You're not on the guest list for the coffee. I spent a year hearing "stay anyway" and thinking it was advice for other people, the ones with the real chairs. It was about me not leaving the room while I was still sitting in it. That's embarrassing. Good. Embarrassment means I noticed.

SHAME: Noticed is not repaired.

ADAM: Noticed is the first bill I actually opened. The repair is boring. Text back the same day. No fake traffic. No calm down. No standing behind Elena. No applause for a blind. If I want credit for doing it, you've already won. Sit in the hall.

ADAM: I'm going to let them interrupt, because a monologue is how I hide. Short. No speeches.

SARAH: If you're going to be fine, at least admit it's a costume.

MARCUS: Stay. Don't bring pottery.

ELENA: Write down that you're still here. Don't call it suspicious.

DAVID: Don't only love your sunny days.

PRIYA: Lateness isn't the personality. It's the runtime.

TOM: Celebrate the flat Tuesday or don't bother.

AISHA: Don't heckle yourself. It's the same as calm down.

LIAM: Wait twenty minutes before you publish the tragedy.

SOPHIE: Don't join the witness. Not even against you.

JORDAN: No graph.

MEI: Small is not nothing. Move your own table.

ADAM: Fear. Shame. One question each. Then I'm done being interviewed by my own bad habits.

FEAR: What happens if you need them and they are busy.

ADAM: Then they are busy. Busy is not abandonment. Marcus taught me that tired can be a lie, and also that a real Tuesday still counts. I can ask. They can say not today. The world continues. You called that a catastrophe because it kept me small and useful to you.

SHAME: What happens if you tell the truth and someone thinks you're too much.

ADAM: Then I am too much for that hour. Liam survives that. Mei survives that. I can survive being a person instead of a host. Too much is not the same as unworthy. You glued those words together. I'm ungluing them on the way out.

FEAR: We'll be back in the morning.

ADAM: Sit in the hall. The coffee is still getting made. That's the contract. You don't get the veto.

ADAM: Before the close, one ordinary instruction I can keep when I'm not feeling profound. Text back on the day. Don't tease a check. Don't invent traffic. Don't stand behind anyone. Don't say calm down. Add the second sentence. Move the table. Say the name if I'm asked. Sit on my side of the bench. Make the coffee. That's not a brand. That's a week. Fear can grade it. I'm not waiting for the grade to start.

FEAR: We'll still mark you down.

ADAM: Mark away. The kettle's on.

SHAME: And if you slip.

ADAM: Then I slip and I don't turn the slip into an identity. A hard season can describe a day. It doesn't get the house. I heard that twelve times. I'm finally including myself in the audience.

FEAR: They'll still be in the room.

ADAM: So will I. That's the only ending I'm interested in.

SHAME: You'll fail a week.

ADAM: Then I'll fail a week and still make the coffee. I heard that from better people than me. I'm done pretending I was the exception.

---

**THE CHAIR ANSWERS BACK**

FEAR: You still haven't introduced us properly. You've been hiding behind your friends' better lines.

ADAM: Fine. Fear sits forward, interrupts, calls it preparation. Shame sits back and takes notes on every joke, in case the joke was a confession I didn't mean to make. Together you are a small production company. You have notes on every episode. Most of the notes are don't.

SHAME: The notes kept you employable.

ADAM: The notes kept me vague. Sarah needed belief, not a tease. Marcus needed a chair, not traffic. Elena needed me out of the doorway. David needed Wednesday. Priya needed a real time. Tom needed the dishes, not the trailer. Aisha needed boring. Liam needed sauce. Sophie needed the subject changed. Jordan needed the name at the right size. Mei needed the corner without a review. I brought personality. They needed a behaviour. Personality was my costume. Behaviour is the apology.

FEAR: List the behaviours again. You'll forget them by Friday.

ADAM: Text back the same day. Don't tease a check. Don't invent a reason. Don't stand behind someone. Don't say calm down. Add the second sentence. Move the table. Ask before you sit in the chair. Follow the subject off the glass. Celebrate the flat Tuesday. Let a no be a no without a tribunal. Make the coffee. If I do half of those, I'm still a better friend than the host who thought insight was the same as care.

SHAME: Half is a fail.

ADAM: Half is a week I can repeat. You only respect transformations. My friends were very clear that transformations are a sales pitch. I'm buying the repeatable week. You can keep the rebrand.

FEAR: And when you want to cancel because we've made the room loud.

ADAM: I say I'm out, not personal, the chair stays. Marcus wrote that sentence. I'm stealing it with credit. Cancelling with the truth is not the same as disappearing with a migraine. You loved the migraine. It made you look like weather instead of a choice. I'm choosing.

SHAME: They'll notice you choosing. Noticing is exposure.

ADAM: Noticing is the show. I spent a season asking other people to be seen. I don't get to leave my own chair empty and call it producing. Sit in the hall. I'm pouring the coffee. You can hear it. That's as much access as you get tonight.

FEAR: We'll be earlier tomorrow.

ADAM: Be early. The kettle will still be louder.

---

The illnesses, the grief, the ex with the key, the clipboard, the landlord, the guard — they may occupy rooms. They may rearrange the furniture. They may turn the lights off. They may convince a person there is no door.

They are not the whole house.

A hard season can describe a life without becoming the identity.

The other person may always be in the room.

They don't necessarily get to decide who lives there.

I'm Adam James. Thank you for sitting in these rooms. If you need someone: in Australia, Lifeline, thirteen eleven fourteen. Beyond Blue, thirteen hundred twenty-two forty-six thirty-six.
